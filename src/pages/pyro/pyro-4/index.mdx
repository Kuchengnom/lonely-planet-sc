Pyro IV's surface is a study in contrasts. The northern hemisphere is dominated by temperate highlands — rolling plains of iron-rich soil that support a sparse but tenacious ecosystem of lichens and scrub. The southern hemisphere, by contrast, is a wasteland of cracked mud flats and salt pans, baked by the system's red star. Travel between the two is done either by ship or by the ancient rail lines that cross the equator — relics of a brief period when Pyro IV was seriously considered for colonization.

<AttractionEntry number={1} title="The Mud Flats" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/pyro/pyro-4-mud-flats.webp" imageAlt="Geometric cracked mud flat patterns on Pyro IV viewed from low orbit">

The Mud Flats are Pyro IV's most distinctive feature — a region the size of a small continent where the soil is so rich in iron oxide that it looks rust-red from orbit. When the occasional rain falls, the flats turn into a mirror of liquid copper. When they dry, they crack into geometric patterns that look almost deliberate.

The flats are also treacherous. The surface looks solid but can be a thin crust over thick mud. Several ships have landed only to sink up to their landing gear. The locals mark safe landing zones with signal buoys, but the markers get stolen or moved. Never trust a signal buoy you didn't place yourself.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="The Overlook" category="hidden-find" image="/images/pyro/pyro-4-overlook.webp" imageAlt="View from the Overlook plateau spanning both hemispheres of Pyro IV">

The Overlook is a natural plateau at the highest point on Pyro IV — a flat-topped mesa that rises above the surrounding highlands. From its edge, you can see both the temperate north and the arid south: a line drawn across the landscape like a border between two worlds.

The plateau has no official name, no structures, and no infrastructure. It is just a flat piece of high ground with a spectacular view. The best time to visit is during the planet's red hour — the brief period each day when the red star is low on the horizon and the entire landscape turns the color of rust. It only lasts about twenty minutes, but it is the closest thing Pyro has to a reliable attraction.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={3} title="The Salvage Yards" category="settlement" image="/images/pyro/pyro-4-salvage-yards.webp" imageAlt="The sprawling Salvage Yards trading post spread across a valley on Pyro IV">

The Salvage Yards are not a single settlement but a patchwork of interconnected scrap heaps, workshops, and black-market trading posts spread across a broad valley in the northern hemisphere. It is said that you can build an entire ship from the parts in the Yards, given enough time, money, and tolerance for things that might explode.

The Yards are run by a loose council of salvage captains and scrap dealers who maintain order through mutual interest. Disputes are settled by verbal duels — formal, public arguments that serve as the Yards' primary form of entertainment. The best deals are not at the main market but at the back row, where dealers sell items that are technically illegal, morally questionable, or both.

</AttractionEntry>

<TouristTip>

- **Mud Flat Landings**: Never land on the Mud Flats without a ground survey. Probe the surface before committing your landing gear. The flats have swallowed ships.
- **The Yards Protocol**: Don't touch anything at the Salvage Yards that isn't in front of a stall. Picking up items without permission is considered theft. Theft is dealt with directly, without involving anyone with a badge.
- **Water**: Pyro IV has trace surface water in the highland regions during the wet season, but it's not potable without heavy filtration. Bring your own supply.
- **Rail Lines**: The old colonial rail lines still function, after a fashion. They're slower than flying and more dangerous than walking, but they'll get you across the equator if your ship is grounded.

</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

Pyro IV is a planet of extremes — temperate highlands that could almost be pleasant, and arid lowlands where the sun turns the surface into a skillet. The equatorial regions are a patchwork of scrubland and desert, and the Mud Flats are a trap for the unwary. Your gear depends on where you're going and what you plan to do, but some things are universal: dust protection, navigation aids, and a respect for ground that looks solid but isn't.

<ExplorerView slot="explorer" author="Dax, Salvage Captain, Pyro IV">
Pyro IV doesn't want to kill you. It just doesn't care if you walk into a Mud Flat with a ship that weighs more than the crust can support. The Yards are safe. The Overlook is beautiful. The highlands are tolerable. The Mud Flats are a joke that isn't funny. Know the difference and you'll be fine. The locals here are rough but fair — treat them with respect and they'll point you to the good stuff. Treat them like a mark and you'll leave with nothing.
</ExplorerView>

**Essential gear:**

- **Dust intake guards for your ship** — The Mud Flats and equatorial dust bands generate fine iron-oxide dust that clogs engine intakes and damages moving parts. Dust guards are mandatory for any surface operation on Pyro IV. Without them, your ship's engines will fail from dust ingestion, and recovery is expensive.
- **Surface probe or weight-testing device** — The Mud Flats look solid but can be a thin crust over thick mud. A surface probe lets you test ground stability before landing. Probe before you commit. Always.
- **Wind-resistant outer layer** — The highlands and the Overlook are exposed to strong, constant winds. A wind-resistant jacket with sealed seams keeps you comfortable and protects against the fine iron-oxide dust that gets everywhere.
- **Water supply and purification tablets** — Pyro IV has trace surface water in the highlands during the wet season, but it is never potable without heavy filtration. Bring your own supply and purification tablets as backup. The Salvage Yards sell water at a premium.
- **Navigation compass and printed maps** — The Mud Flats have no landmarks. The Overlook is a single point of reference. Without a compass and map, you will get lost in the highlands, and the locals are not always forthcoming about directions.

**Strongly recommended:**

- **Dust mask or respirator** — The iron-oxide dust is fine enough to penetrate standard masks and irritate the respiratory tract. A respirator with HEPA-grade filtration is the best protection. The dust also scratches lenses and damages camera sensors.
- **Thermal imaging camera** — The contrast between the temperate north and arid south is most dramatic in thermal imaging. The Mud Flats' hidden liquid mud shows clearly on thermal, helping you avoid danger spots and photograph the geometric patterns from a safe distance.
- **Compact first aid kit** — Cuts from oxidized metal, dust-related respiratory issues, and dehydration are the most common medical problems. A well-stocked kit with bandages, antiseptic, painkillers, and electrolyte tablets covers most issues.
- **Barter items (tools, fuel cells, electronics)** — The Salvage Yards operate on a barter economy as much as a credit one. Showing up with something genuinely useful opens doors that credits cannot. Small tools, replacement parts, and electronics move especially well.
- **Portable shade structure** — The equatorial sun is relentless. A portable shade or reflective tarp lets you create temporary shelter during long stays at the Yards or the Overlook. Sunburn on Pyro IV is painful because the red star's UV output, while lower in intensity, penetrates dust differently and can cause unexpected damage.

<TouristTip>
  The Mud Flats are the single most dangerous feature on Pyro IV. They have swallowed ships. They have swallowed people. They look solid from a distance. Never land on the Mud Flats without a surface probe and a ground survey. If you see a signal buoy marking a safe landing zone, verify it yourself before committing — the markers get stolen or moved. The locals will tell you this with a straight face and a smile. Believe them.
</TouristTip>
