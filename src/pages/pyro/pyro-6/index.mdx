Pyro VI is a classic gas giant — massive, turbulent, and utterly hostile to anything resembling life. Its atmosphere is a stew of hydrogen, helium, and traces of ammonia, methane, and water vapor. The pressure increases exponentially as you descend, and the temperature ranges from -150°C at the cloud tops to thousands of degrees at the core. The planet's most striking feature is its storm bands — parallel bands of clouds that move at different speeds, creating spectacular turbulence. The largest, the Great Scarlet Spot, is a storm the size of a small moon that has been raging for at least a century.

### The Moons of Pyro VI

Pyro VI has a retinue of moons, ranging from captured asteroids to a few small terrestrial bodies. None are particularly hospitable, but some have strategic value. Pyro VIb is a small rocky moon with a thin atmosphere and a surface covered in impact craters — and the site of a long-abandoned research station, the Pyro VI Orbital Observatory, which studied the gas giant's storm patterns before funding ran out. The moons are popular with scavengers and pirates: the former looking for salvage, the latter looking for hideouts.

<AttractionEntry number={1} title="Gas Mining Operations" category="hidden-find" image="/images/pyro/pyro-6-mining.webp" imageAlt="Gas mining platforms floating in Pyro VI's upper atmosphere">

The gas mining platforms of Pyro VI are marvels of engineering — massive structures that float in the planet's upper atmosphere, held aloft by buoyancy and kept in place by powerful thrusters. They harvest hydrogen and helium from the atmosphere, process it into fuel, and ship it throughout the system.

The platforms are dangerous places. The storms are frequent and violent, and a platform that drifts too deep into the atmosphere will be crushed by the pressure. Workers are well-paid for the risk, but turnover is high. For those willing to take the risk, the pay is substantial — and the view, a sea of churning clouds stretching to an impossibly distant horizon, is unlike anything else in the system.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="The Rings" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/pyro/pyro-6-rings.webp" imageAlt="Pyro VI's spectacular ice and rock ring system lit by the red star">

Pyro VI's ring system is one of the most beautiful in the known systems. Composed of ice and rock particles ranging from dust to boulders the size of small ships, the rings stretch for hundreds of thousands of kilometers. In the light of Pyro's red star, they glow with a warm, almost surreal amber hue.

The rings are also dangerous. The particles move at high speed, and a ship that enters the rings without proper shielding will be sandblasted to scrap. But for those who come prepared — parked safely in a Lagrangian point — the rings are a breathtaking sight: a river of light that circles the planet like a cosmic wedding band.

</AttractionEntry>

## What to Bring

Pyro VI is a gas giant — there is no surface to land on, and no conventional tourism exists here. What "visitors" do is observe from orbit, photograph the rings, and occasionally tour the gas mining platforms if they have corporate authorization. Your gear list reflects the fact that you are never more than a few hundred kilometers from a gravity well that can crush your ship, a radiation field that can fry your electronics, and an atmosphere that contains enough ammonia to melt your eyes out of your skull.


**Essential gear (orbital operations):**

- **Radiation shielding for your ship** — The gas giant's radiation belt is intense and can damage unshielded electronics. Your ship's radiation shielding should be rated for prolonged exposure to gas giant radiation environments. Check all sensitive electronics before and after approach.
- ** Maneuvering thruster fuel reserve (minimum 40%)** — Navigating near the rings or approaching a mining platform requires constant thruster burns. A minimum 40% fuel reserve for maneuvering ensures you can respond to debris collisions, platform approach corrections, or emergency departures. Do not arrive with less than half a tank.
- **Gravity well egress vector calculator** — Pyro VI's gravity extends far beyond the visible atmosphere. You must have a calculated quantum jump egress vector before entering the outer ring system. This is not optional — running calculations while falling is a death sentence.
- **Debris impact detection system** — The ring system contains particles from dust to boulder-sized rock, all moving at high relative velocity. A debris detection system alerts you to close encounters and lets you maneuver before impact. Ring navigation is not a straight line — it is a constant dance with unpredictable hazards.

**Essential gear (platform visits, if authorized):**

- **Pressurized environment suit (rated for gas giant atmosphere)** — The mining platforms float in Pyro VI's upper atmosphere. While the platforms themselves are pressurized, any exterior work or emergency evacuation requires a suit rated for the ambient pressure and ammonia-helium composition. Standard EVA suits are adequate; full gas giant-rated suits are preferred.
- **Ammonia-resistant seals and gaskets** — The ammonia in the atmosphere degrades standard seals over time. If you are working on or near a platform for an extended period, spare ammonia-resistant seals are important for maintaining equipment integrity.
- **Heavy-duty communication equipment** — The gas giant's atmospheric interference degrades standard communications. High-gain antennas and frequency-hopping transceivers maintain contact with orbiting ships during platform operations.

**Strongly recommended (for ring photography):**

- **High-speed camera with vibration damping** — Photographing the rings requires fast shutter speeds to freeze the motion of the particles and vibration damping to counteract the constant thruster corrections needed for stable positioning. A gimbal-stabilized camera system is ideal.
- **Polarizing filter** — The rings' amber glow is most dramatic with polarized light. A variable polarizer lets you adjust the filter as your angle to the rings changes during approach and departure.
- **Heat-resistant clothing** — If the mining platforms are accessible, the interior temperatures can be high due to the processing equipment. Heat-resistant clothing is comfortable and practical for extended platform visits.
- **Portable radiation dosimeter** — Prolonged exposure to the gas giant's radiation belt can exceed safe limits. A personal dosimeter tracks your cumulative exposure and alerts you when it's time to move to a safer position or return to your ship.

<TouristTip>

The gravity well extends far, the radiation is intense, and the rings are a constant hazard. Arrive with calculated egress vectors, full maneuvering fuel reserves, and a healthy respect for the physics involved. Book platform visits through the corporate transit office in advance — unauthorized approaches will get you turned away at best. The rings are worth the planning.
</TouristTip>
