Pyro II's surface is a geologist's fever dream. Massive lava plains stretch for hundreds of kilometers, their surfaces cracked and cooled into intricate patterns that look almost deliberate from orbit. Ancient lava tubes snake beneath the surface, some large enough to fly a small ship through. The real danger, though, is the seismic activity — surface quakes are common enough that most outposts are built on shock-absorbing foundations.

<AttractionEntry number={1} title="The Glass Fields" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/pyro/pyro-2-glass.webp" imageAlt="Vast volcanic glass fields of Pyro II reflecting the red star">

The Glass Fields are exactly what they sound like: vast expanses of volcanic glass, formed when lava flows met ancient lakebeds and cooled instantly. The result is a landscape of shimmering, razor-sharp obsidian that reflects the system's red star in a thousand directions. It is breathtaking and lethal in equal measure.

The glass is sharp enough to shred most EVA suits, and the reflective surface can disorient pilots who try to land without proper terrain mapping. But for those who come prepared, the Glass Fields are one of the most visually stunning landscapes in the system. Some ultra-luxury tourism companies have started offering guided walks across specially treated paths. The locals laugh at this, but they cash the checks.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="The Spine" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/pyro/pyro-2-spine.webp" imageAlt="Volcanic spire ridge of the Spine cutting across Pyro II's northern hemisphere">

The Spine is a geological anomaly — a chain of volcanic spires that stretches for over 4,000 kilometers across the northern hemisphere. The spires range from a few hundred meters to over two kilometers in height, and their bases are honeycombed with lava tubes and caves. Some of these caves have been expanded by miners and smugglers into makeshift bases and hideouts.

The Spine is also a navigational landmark. Pilots use it to orient themselves when approaching Pyro II, and its distinctive profile is visible from orbit. "Follow the Spine to the Heart" is a common saying among Pyro pilots, referring to the planet's most active volcanic region at the spine's southern terminus. The Spine Ridge habs, built into the rock itself, are the closest thing the planet has to permanent civilization.

</AttractionEntry>

<TouristTip>

- **Glass Field Navigation**: Never attempt to cross the Glass Fields without terrain mapping loaded. The reflective surface creates visual confusion, and a wrong step means shredded suit seals.
- **Seismic Awareness**: Ground quakes are frequent and largely unpredictable. Anchor your ship to hardened basalt, not volcanic soil. What holds you today may crack open tomorrow.
- **The Spine Habs**: Transmit on the independent frequency before approaching Spine Ridge. The hab operators are welcoming, but unannounced arrivals occasionally get shot at by nervous neighbors.
- **Lava Tubes**: Tempting for exploration, genuinely dangerous. Many connect to active magma systems deeper down. If the air smells of sulfur inside a tube, back out immediately.

</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

Pyro II is hot enough to make you regret your life choices, toxic enough to make you regret your atmospheric filters, and geologically active enough to make you question the structural integrity of everything you're standing on. It is not as lethal as Pyro I, but it is not kind. Your gear should reflect the fact that this planet will kill you slowly, painfully, and with a variety of different methods depending on your mood.

<ExplorerView slot="explorer" author="Tarn Voss, Independent Miner & Refueling Depot Operator">
Pyro II is the planet that keeps you humble. You can survive Pyro I because you have the gear. You can survive Pyro V because you have the patience. But Pyro II just keeps throwing things at you — heat, quakes, glass that can shred a suit, lava tubes that can swallow a rover, and a population that won't warn you about any of it. Come prepared, stay alert, and don't trust the ground.
</ExplorerView>

**Essential gear:**

- **Thermal-resistant EVA suit (rated to 200°C+)** — While Pyro II's surface is cooler than Pyro I's, it still regularly exceeds 200°C in sun-exposed areas. A thermal-resistant suit with active cooling is the minimum. Standard EVA suits will degrade rapidly and may fail during prolonged surface operations.
- **Sealed environmental suit with chemical filtration** — The thin toxic atmosphere contains sulfur compounds and other corrosive gases. A sealed suit with chemical-grade filtration protects both your respiratory system and your suit's internal electronics from corrosive damage.
- **Shock-absorbing footwear with reinforced soles** — The Glass Fields will shred unprotected boots. The volcanic soil is abrasive and sharp in places. Reinforced soles with anti-puncture properties are essential. The glass here is volcanic obsidian — it is sharper than any manufactured blade.
- **Seismic early-warning detector** — Pyro II experiences frequent ground quakes with little warning. A handheld seismic detector gives you seconds or minutes of advance notice, which can be the difference between a bruised ego and a broken body. Mount one on your ship for continuous monitoring.
- **Emergency beacon with extended range** — The distance between the Spine Ridge habs and any other settlement is vast. If you're stranded, rescue is hours away at minimum. An extended-range beacon (500+ km) gives you the best chance of being located.

**Strongly recommended:**

- **Terrain mapping system with real-time hazard overlay** — The Glass Fields and lava tube entrances are invisible hazards. A terrain mapping system with hazard overlay helps you navigate safely and avoid the most dangerous areas.
- **Thermal imaging camera** — Lava tubes, active vents, and cooling lava flows are all much easier to identify with thermal imaging. It helps you avoid hot spots and photograph the more spectacular features from a safe distance.
- **Compact first aid kit with burn treatment supplies** — Burns from hot ground, glass cuts, and seismic injuries are the most common medical issues on Pyro II. A well-stocked first aid kit with burn dressings, antiseptic, and painkillers is essential.
- **Tether line and anchor points** — The glass fields are deceptively slippery, and strong winds can knock you off balance. A tether line with anchor points lets you secure yourself while working or photographing in exposed areas.
- **Spare suit seals and repair kit** — Glass is unforgiving. A scratch on your suit that looks minor can become a catastrophic breach in minutes. A repair kit with sealant and spare seals is a must.

<TouristTip>
  The Glass Fields are beautiful and deadly. They are also the most photographed location on Pyro II, which means tourists regularly underestimate them. Never walk across the Glass Fields without reinforced boots and a guide who knows the safe routes. The glass here has cut through standard EVA boot soles like paper. If your boots aren't rated for volcanic terrain, you don't walk on the Glass Fields. Period.
</TouristTip>
