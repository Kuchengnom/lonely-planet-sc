Pyro V's surface is a frozen wasteland — a mix of ice plains, frozen methane lakes, and low mountain ranges that look like they were carved by a sculptor who gave up halfway through. The atmosphere is thin and contains trace amounts of toxic gases, making prolonged exposure dangerous without an EVA suit. The planet's most striking feature is its network of frozen methane lakes — vast, shallow depressions that fill with liquid methane during the brief summer period, then freeze solid, creating mirror-like surfaces that reflect the sky with eerie clarity.

<AttractionEntry number={1} title="The Amber Lakes" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/pyro/pyro-5-amber-lakes.webp" imageAlt="Frozen amber methane lakes glowing in Pyro's red starlight">

The Amber Lakes are Pyro V's most beautiful feature — a series of frozen methane lakes that stretch across the planet's equatorial region. In the light of the red star, the frozen surfaces glow with a warm, amber light that looks like liquid gold. It is a sight that has made many a jaded pilot catch their breath.

The lakes are also dangerous. The ice is thin in places, and the frozen methane can be unstable. Several ships have landed on what looked like solid ground, only to sink through the ice into freezing liquid below. The best views are from the rim of the great equatorial rift — a canyon system that cuts across the planet's midsection — where the lakes spread out below like a mosaic of amber tiles.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="The Pole Station" category="settlement" image="/images/pyro/pyro-5-pole-station.webp" imageAlt="The research outpost cluster at Pyro V's south magnetic pole">

The Pole Station is a small research outpost at Pyro V's south magnetic pole, established by an independent research collective to study the planet's unusual magnetic field. The station is modest — a cluster of prefab habs connected by pressurized corridors — but the research it produces is surprisingly important. The magnetic anomalies here have implications for ship navigation throughout the system, and the data collected is used by pilots across Pyro.

The station's residents are scientists, graduate students, and support staff who have chosen to live in one of the system's least hospitable locations. They are generally welcoming to visitors, though they tend to judge you based on how much you know about magnetohydrodynamics. The station's common room has a small bar that serves what might be the best hot chocolate in the system — a closely guarded recipe rumored to involve real cacao.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={3} title="The Frozen City" category="hidden-find" image="/images/pyro/pyro-5-frozen-city.webp" imageAlt="Mysterious ancient ruins frozen in the northern hemisphere of Pyro V">

The Frozen City is a collection of ruins in the planet's northern hemisphere — structures made of an unknown material that have survived exposure to the elements for an unknown length of time. The ruins are not particularly large — perhaps the size of a small town — but their origin is a mystery. They are too old to be human, and their design doesn't match any known alien architecture.

The ruins have attracted a small but dedicated community of researchers, treasure hunters, and conspiracy theorists. Some believe the city predates the system itself. Others think it was built by a lost human colony. Everyone agrees that it is eerie, fascinating, and probably not entirely safe to explore alone.

</AttractionEntry>

<TouristTip>

- **Ice Landing**: Never land on the methane lakes. They look solid and are not. Use the hardened plains near the Pole Station or the rocky plateaus above the equatorial rift.
- **Thermal Management**: The temperature on Pyro V will find every weak point in your ship's insulation. Landing gear heating is not optional — frozen gear means you're staying until someone comes to get you.
- **Magnetic Interference**: The planetary magnetic anomalies will interfere with standard navigation systems in the polar regions. The Pole Station beacon is your primary nav reference; don't rely on your onboard compass.
- **The Frozen City**: Never explore the ruins alone. The structures are unstable in ways that aren't obvious until something falls on you, and no one will hear you call for help. The Frozen City sits roughly 30 km northeast of Pole Station on the polar plateau — a city carved entirely from ice with no known origin. See the [full PHDS exploration guide](/lonely-planet-sc/pyro/phds/locations/frozen-city-ruins/) for coordinates, navigation, and safety requirements.

</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

Pyro V is the system's freezer — a frozen world where the temperature never rises above your ship's minimum operating threshold, the atmosphere is thin and toxic, and the surface is locked in permanent winter. This is not a world for casual visitors. This is a world for scientists, solitude-seekers, and people who need to think about something other than the last three years of their lives. The beauty is undeniable. The cold is relentless.

<ExplorerView slot="explorer" author="Dr. Chen, Magnetohydrodynamics Researcher, Pole Station">
I've been here three years. The cold doesn't bother me anymore — it's the isolation that gets you. The beauty here is the kind that makes you feel small in a way that's almost religious. The Amber Lakes at sunset. The magnetic auroras in the polar sky. The Frozen City standing there like a question mark in the ice. You come here to think. You leave with answers you didn't know you were looking for. Just make sure you bring enough thermal suits before you go.
</ExplorerView>

**Essential gear:**

- **Thermal survival suit (rated to -150°C minimum)** — Pyro V's surface temperature can drop well below -150°C in shaded areas. Standard EVA suits are marginally functional but will not provide adequate protection for prolonged surface operations. A thermal survival suit rated to at least -150°C is the minimum; -200°C is the recommendation. This is the same class of suit used on MicroTech — if you have gear for one, it serves both.
- **Heated suit liner (battery-powered)** — The cold on Pyro V is so extreme that passive insulation alone is insufficient. A heated suit liner with a rated battery life of at least 12 hours (at maximum output) is essential. Battery performance degrades in extreme cold, so bring spares.
- **Thermal goggles with sealed edges and anti-fog coating** — Breath condensation freezes instantly on Pyro V. Anti-fog coatings are essential. Sealed edges protect against the thin but toxic atmosphere. Polarized lenses reduce glare from the ice and frozen methane surfaces.
- **Emergency beacon with extended range** — The Pole Station is the only permanent settlement on Pyro V. If you're stranded, rescue is measured in days, not hours. An extended-range beacon (500+ km) is critical. The magnetic anomalies also interfere with some navigation systems, making precise beacon coordinates even more important.
- **Thermally insulated water flask** — Standard water bladders freeze solid within minutes. A vacuum-insulated flask keeps water drinkable for hours and can serve as a heat source in an emergency. Fill it before you leave any sheltered area.
- **Magnetic anomaly detector or compass with calibration** — The planetary magnetic field is so strong and variable that standard navigation aids can become unreliable. A dedicated magnetometer or a compass that can be recalibrated for the local field is necessary for navigation outside the Pole Station's immediate vicinity.

**Strongly recommended:**

- **Thermal imaging camera** — The frozen methane lakes glow with a warm amber light that is visible on thermal imaging. The camera also helps identify thin ice, active thermal vents beneath the ice, and safe routes across the frozen surface.
- **Axe or ice pick** — The frozen methane lakes are deceptive. An axe lets you test ice thickness as you walk and serves as an emergency tool for self-rescue if you fall through. It is not a drill — several pilots have fallen through thin ice and been retrieved by the Pole Station crew.
- **Compact first aid kit with frostbite treatment supplies** — Frostbite is the most common serious medical emergency on Pyro V. A kit with rapid-warming patches, frostbite ointment, and pain management medication is essential. Rewarming frozen tissue requires careful procedure — incorrect treatment causes permanent damage.
- **Radiosonde or portable weather station** — The weather on Pyro V is stable but severe. A portable weather station helps you understand wind patterns, temperature gradients, and magnetic conditions that affect navigation.
- **Extra clothing layers (merino wool or synthetic)** — Cotton is useless here. Merino wool or synthetic insulation layers provide warmth even when damp and resist the cold better than most materials. Bring at least two spare layers — if your primary suit fails, these buy you time.
- **Solar panels with cold-weather rating** — The red star's light is weak, but on Pyro V, it's the only power source available for extended surface operations. Cold-weather-rated solar panels can supplement your battery supply for multi-day expeditions.

<TouristTip>
  The most underestimated danger on Pyro V is not the cold — it's the isolation. The Pole Station is a small facility with limited medical resources. If you need a doctor, you get one, but "one" may not be enough. Carry everything you might need in your own kit. The best advice from the station staff: never leave the station without telling someone where you're going, when you'll be back, and what you're taking with you. If you don't come back when you said you would, they will send a search party. But it takes time. Don't make them wait.
</TouristTip>
