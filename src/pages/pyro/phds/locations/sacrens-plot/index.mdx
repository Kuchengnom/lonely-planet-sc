<TouristTip>

**Sacren's Plot is private property, and it is treated that way.** Citizens for Prosperity posts guards, turrets, and missiles around the approach. "Approachable on foot, by vehicle, by ship" describes the terrain, not the reception. The guns are there to hold the outpost against people like the ones the CFP was founded to hunt down, and nobody mistakes a trader for one of them. Bring a reason to be there, or do not come.

Sacren's Plot is a fortified outpost wedged into a red rock canyon in the temperate highlands of Pyro IV, held by the Citizens for Prosperity — the frontier organisation that has spent decades trying to tame Pyro's outlaw trade. It is one of a scatter of CFP-held posts across the system, and one of the very few places on this planet where a traveller can arrive unarmed and still be left alone.<TextAttribution dataType="description" locationName="Sacren's Plot" />

Sacren's Plot is built into the rock rather than spread across it. The canyon walls rise on either side, the outcrop above the main structures carries a communications mast, and the approach funnels down a single dry channel that a competent sentry can cover end to end. From the air, on the descent, it looks less like a settlement and more like a gap in the mountains that someone has moved a building into.

The name follows the CFP's own convention — a person, then a place. Chawla's Beach on the other side of this planet works the same way, and so do the posts on Monox and Bloom. Locals use the Farro Data Centres by their number instead, with the flat tone of someone reciting a filing cabinet.

## The Canyon

The approach is a dry channel between two sandstone walls that narrows as you go in. It is drivable and, in the right places, landable, but the walls are the reason the outpost exists. A hull that comes down the channel fast will find the far wall sooner than it finds the ground.

Higher up, on the outcrop, the mast is visible from a long way out — a thin vertical line against the plateau. It is the single most useful thing to look for on the approach, because the canyon itself is not visible until you are inside it.

Inside the canyon, the ground is hardpan over old sediment, rutted by decades of ground vehicles. There are cut ledges along the walls where structures have been widened, and the rock is scarred in a few places in a way that is unmistakably the result of excavation rather than erosion. The first builders of this place cut into the rock instead of hauling material out to it, and every generation since has simply built on top of what was already there.

## Inside the Wire

The main structure is a long, low building set back against the eastern wall, with a covered market row running along its frontage. Beyond that the settlement steps uphill: storage, workshop, a freight elevator into a cut shaft that goes down into the rock, and the tower that gives the place its silhouette on the ridge.

Everything is inside the perimeter, and the perimeter is not subtle. Guard towers cover the channel. Turrets are mounted on the wall cuts. Missiles are mounted on the higher ground, on the outcrop, aimed down the one line of approach the terrain allows. This is a place that has been attacked and has decided not to be attacked again — the fortification is a scar, and everyone here knows it.

The market row is the part worth visiting. It is small, orderly, and stocked: the kind of supply line that only exists where someone has negotiated a working relationship with a mining operation and a fleet of freighters. Storage kiosks sit along the row, and trade terminals handle the paperwork. There are harvestables to be had, and a mechanic's shop that will work on hulls — though whether they will work on yours depends entirely on who you are.

## Citizens for Prosperity

The faction that holds the plot is the point of interest. Citizens for Prosperity began as "Citizens for Pyro" — a civilian organisation with a narrow, unglamorous mission: hunting down the criminals who had turned the Pyro system into a refuge, and doing something about it. The name changed when the work changed. What started as chasing criminals became a resettlement programme — moving people out of the places the outlaws had taken, and putting them somewhere with a functioning roof.

The organisation is not a government and does not claim to be one. It keeps a garrison, a market, and a set of outposts, and it asks the minimum of the people under its roof. Its headquarters sit in Lorville and Hurston on Stanton, and it maintains posts across Pyro — Monox, Bloom, Ignis, Terminus, and here on Pyro IV. Chawla's Beach on this planet is the other CFP site on the roster.

Their reputation on Pyro IV is sharply better than the reputational arithmetic of "armed frontier militia" would suggest. The CFP does not tax travellers. It does not raid. It runs a trade terminal, it keeps a market, it keeps a garrison, and it asks you to leave the same way you came. That is a functioning arrangement, and on a planet where the alternatives are "the Yards' verbal duels" and "Ruin Station's shooting gallery", functioning is worth a great deal.

What the CFP will not do is take kindly to anyone working for a corporation, anyone carrying corporate security contracts, or anyone looking to buy what they should not have. The organisation exists to end the outlaw trade, and it makes no pretence about who it is for. Citizens for Prosperity is a statement about who the outpost is for, and it is not a subtle one.

## The Farro Data Centres

Sacren's Plot is not alone in the highlands. Ten Farro Data Centres — I through X — are scattered across Pyro IV's northern and equatorial regions, all of them converted, all of them far larger than anything at Sacren's. They were built as data processing and relay installations for an industrial operation that no longer exists; what they became after that is a matter of faction.

The distinction matters when you are planning a route. The Farro installations are major sites, heavily garrisoned, and they are not places to improvise. Sacren's is a single outpost with a market and a gate. If you want to see one of them and get out alive, pick the smaller one first and learn how the CFP works before you walk into the larger one.

## Visiting

There is a route that works, and it is not the obvious one. Quantum to Pyro, then to Pyro IV. Set the public beacon for 04S-WL29 and let the nav computer bring you over the highlands. The last stretch is canyon, and the last stretch is where you want to be slow and high. A surface vehicle will get you to the gate; a ship can put down in the channel if you read the rock correctly, and if you cannot, there is a flat pan a little way out where you can set down and walk.

Inside, the market row is open to anyone with a reason to be there. The trade terminals take UEC. Storage kiosks hold freight, and the freight is worth looking at. The freight elevator descends into the rock and does not come back up with you in a hurry.

What to bring: dust protection. Pyro IV's atmosphere is thin and only marginally breathable, and the canyon traps whatever the wind carries. UEC for the terminals. A local introduction, if you can get one — the Salvage Yards can make it, and Dax on Pyro IV can be reached through them. And a story. The gate guards are not paid to be hostile, but they are paid to be thorough, and "I am just looking" is not an answer that opens a gate on a planet run by a frontier militia.

## What to Leave Behind

Do not photograph the defences. Do not ask about the missile emplacements, and do not be seen looking at them. Do not offer to carry freight in either direction without understanding who is paying — on Pyro IV, running cargo for one side is a declaration of loyalty to them.

Most of all, do not treat it as a free site. It is a business with a garrison, and the customer who treats it as a free site is the reason it has a garrison.
