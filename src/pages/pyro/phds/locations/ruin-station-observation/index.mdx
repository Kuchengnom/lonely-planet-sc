Ruin Station sits in orbit around Terminus (Pyro VI), the frozen outer planet of the Pyro system. What you see when you look at the station from your approach vector is a sprawling, ugly, fascinating mess of ship hulls, station modules, and improvised additions bolted together over nearly five centuries. It looks like a junkyard decided to build a space station and then forgot to stop.

The station's location was deliberately chosen by its original builders — Pyrotechnic Amalgamated, who founded it in 2506 under the name MacEwan Station. The site was selected for its relative safety from flares from the star Pyro, which made construction on Monox (Pyro II) and Bloom (Pyro III) risky. It was named after the watch officer of the *Roustabout*, the ship whose scan records led PyAm to discover the Pyro system in the first place.

By the time the station was completed in 2512, PyAm was already seeing diminishing returns from the system. The company abandoned MacEwan Station in the 2550s after stripping it of valuable parts, declared bankruptcy in 2563, and left the crumbling structure to whoever wanted it. That was the outlaws.

## What You Can See

### The Observation Position

From the station's exterior walkways and upper-level windows, you have a vantage point that few places in the verse can match. You're looking down at the star Pyro from the outer reaches of the system — a view that compresses twelve years of travel from Stanton into a single glance at a system where planets are actively trying to kill you.

From this position, you can see:

- **Pyro (the star)**: A violent, unpredictable star that drives the entire system's geology. From Terminus's orbit, it appears smaller than it does from the inner planets but no less dangerous. The flares that make Bloom's surface scorched are just distant sparks from this angle.
- **The inner planets**: Bloom (Pyro III), Monox (Pyro II), and Pyro I are all visible from this vantage point, though the glare from Pyro makes the inner system difficult to observe in detail without specialized equipment.
- **The Pyro Belt**: The asteroid belt between Pyro III and Pyro IV is visible as a thin line of reflected light, a band of debris that separates the rocky inner system from the gas giants and outer planets.
- **Terminus itself**: The planet below the station is a frozen, desolate world — cold enough that its atmosphere has largely frozen onto the surface. From orbit, it appears as a dull gray sphere with faint hints of subsurface ice deposits.

### The Ghost Arena

Ruin Station houses the Ghost Arena, a combat arena built into the station's larger hollowed-out modules. Spectators pack into whatever seating the station's improvised infrastructure can provide — usually welded-together shipping containers bolted to the floor. The fights are brutal, the betting is serious, and the security is nonexistent.

The arena itself is a relic from the station's earliest days as an outlaw gathering point. When squatters and runaways first began occupying the abandoned PyAm station, they filled its vast interior volumes with makeshift structures. The arena occupies one of the largest of these volumes — a hollowed-out section of the station that once housed mining equipment before PyAm stripped it out.

### The Exterior Walkways

The station's exterior walkways are the only place you can get truly unobstructed views of the system. They are also the most dangerous part of the station, as they're essentially open to space with minimal shielding and no cover. If the station is under attack — which happens, frequently — the walkways are the first places people die.

The walkways connect various modules of the station and pass by some of the most visually striking features: bolted-on observation domes, exterior weapon mounts that look like they were salvaged from decommissioned military vessels, and the massive docking bays where ships arrive and depart with what can generously be described as irregularity.

## Who Controls Ruin Now

XenoThreat is the current pack controlling the station, according to recent Advocacy reports. Rough & Ready exercises jurisdiction over the station, but control can shift at a moment's notice. The station has been through numerous handovers since PyAm abandoned it — squatters, runaways, black-market operators, and eventually organized outlaw groups who understood that a station this big, this well-located, and this unprotected was a strategic asset.

Where Spider's outlaw culture centers around honor among thieves, Pyro is based on whoever has the most guns wins. Ruin Station reflects this philosophy in its physical structure: every module, every corridor, every piece of equipment has been modified, reinforced, or replaced by whoever currently controls the section it's in.

## Why Visit

Let me be clear: **there is no good reason to visit Ruin Station as a tourist**. If you are reading this guide and the phrase "there is no good reason" hasn't dissuaded you, then at least you should understand what you're getting into.

The reasons people visit Ruin Station are:

1. **Information**: The station is an intelligence clearinghouse. Miners, smugglers, pirates, and traders all converge here. Patience and a few rounds of drinks can yield more useful information than months of official reports from Stanton.
2. **The Ghost Arena**: For some people, watching brutal unarmed combat in a dilapidated space station is entertainment. This is a judgment call.
3. **The view**: From the exterior walkways, the Pyro system is visible in a way that no other location in the verse can replicate. The entire star system — a violent, dangerous, geologically unstable mess — spread out below you on a timescale of seconds rather than decades of travel.
4. **Curiosity**: The station is a historical artifact. It was built by one of the most ambitious mining corporations in human history, abandoned in disgrace, and repurposed by the very people that corporation would have employed as low-level labor. The irony is visible in every corroded bulkhead.

## Survival Tips

- **Blend in**: If you stand out — clean clothes, obvious tourist gear, confused expression — you will be noticed. Notice being bad.
- **Don't flash credits**: UEC means nothing to the people running this station. Trade goods, information, and discretion are the currencies that matter.
- **Know when to leave**: Ruin Station's violence is unpredictable. Fights erupt without warning. Gang wars change the safety profile of the station hour by hour. Have an exit strategy and execute it when the situation shifts.
- **The walkways are risky**: If you're not familiar with the local power structure, stay off the exterior walkways. They're the preferred hunting ground for station locals with nothing better to do.
- **XenoThreat controls the station**: Even if Rough & Ready claims jurisdiction, XenoThreat has the guns. Don't make anyone on the station feel threatened.
