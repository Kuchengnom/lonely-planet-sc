The ruins of Pyrotechnic Amalgamated's mining empire still litter Bloom's surface like the bones of a great leviathan — silent, decaying, and slowly being reclaimed by whatever vegetation the planet can coax from its frozen soil. If you want to understand what Pyro was in its golden age, and what it lost when that age ended, these abandoned sites are where you come to find out.

The sites were established during the 2500s, when Pyrotechnic Amalgamated — sometimes called PyAm — was at the height of its operations. The company had discovered the Pyro system in 2493 and immediately made Bloom the center of everything, building staging sites, employee barracks, and ore-processing centers on what was then a pristine icy world. At its peak, these facilities supported thousands of workers mining minerals that powered frontier economies across multiple systems.

Then came the financial collapse of the mid-26th century. Maintenance costs outpaced mining profits, outlaw activity increased, and in 2563, Pyrotechnic Amalgamated declared bankruptcy. They left overnight — or as overnight as you can leave when your operation spans an entire planet. The workers who couldn't afford to return to the UEE homeworlds became squatters, the outlaws who had been keeping their distance came out of hiding, and the settlements that PyAm built became someone else's problem.

## The Main Sites

### Prospect Depot

Prospect Depot was PyAm's primary storage and distribution outpost on Bloom. The in-game description at the time reads: *"Things only get better from here. Traders welcome."* — a cheerful slogan that reads differently when everything else on the planet has been abandoned for centuries.

The depot itself is a storage outpost on Bloom's surface, situated near the settlement zone that was originally built to house PyAm logistics staff. Today it is a collection of decommissioned storage containers and half-constructed buildings that have been repurposed by squatters and scavengers. The structure that once housed ore processing equipment still stands — a skeleton of corrugated metal and rusted piping — and it has been incorporated into the makeshift housing of whatever group has claimed the site recently.

The location is accessible from multiple approach vectors on Bloom, making it one of the easier abandoned sites to reach. The danger rating is moderate — the structure itself is stable enough to enter, and the atmosphere allows for short surface operations without a full EVA suit. The primary risks here are the people who have claimed the site, not the site itself.

### Bueno Ravine

Bueno Ravine was one of Bloom's primary mining operations. Unlike Prospect Depot, which was a logistics hub, Bueno Ravine was an active mining site — deep excavations into Bloom's surface where ore was extracted, sorted, and shipped to processing facilities elsewhere in the system.

The ravine itself is a long, narrow gouge in the planet's surface, the kind of scar that only industrial-scale mining can leave behind. The walls are lined with exposed mineral veins, and the floor is littered with abandoned equipment: crushers that have stopped crushing, conveyor belts that haven't moved in centuries, and drilling rigs whose engines would turn over if you had the parts to jumpstart them.

Bueno Ravine is less accessible than Prospect Depot and the surrounding terrain is rougher, with less stable ground and thinner atmosphere at the depths of the ravine. The site carries a higher difficulty rating, but the mineral deposits here are substantial — anyone with the right equipment could theoretically begin mining again if they could justify the startup costs.

### Carver's Ridge

Carver's Ridge was another mining operation on Bloom, and it shares many characteristics with Bueno Ravine: abandoned equipment, exposed mineral veins, and an atmosphere that requires supplementation at the deeper work areas. The ridge formation itself gives the site its name — the mining operations carved into the side of a broad geological ridge, leaving behind terraced levels that descend into the planet's crust.

What makes Carver's Ridge distinctive is its elevation. The upper levels of the ridge offer panoramic views of Bloom's frozen biomes and the liquid saltwater expanses in more temperate zones — a stark contrast to the claustrophobic darkness of the deeper mining tunnels. For photographers and visual explorers, the ridge provides a unique combination of industrial decay and natural beauty.

## Historical Context

The PyAm mining sites represent a specific moment in frontier history: the transition from corporate-controlled space to ungoverned territory. Before the abandonment, these sites were tightly controlled operations with strict safety protocols, scheduled supply runs, and a chain of command that reached all the way to the company's headquarters on Pyro VI. After the abandonment, they became part of Bloom's new identity — a planet that was no longer an operation but a habitat, for whoever was willing to live among the ruins.

The squatter communities that formed around these sites developed their own economies. Some of the mining equipment was repurposed for small-scale extraction: individual miners and small crews would take over sections of larger sites, working independently and selling their ore through informal channels. Others simply used the infrastructure as shelter, the mining facilities providing ready-made housing in a world where construction materials are expensive and scarce.

The three main gangs operating on Bloom — Citizens for Prosperity, Headhunters, and XenoThreat — all have territories that overlap with or encompass former PyAm sites, and the infrastructure of these abandoned operations is woven into the power structure of the planet. You can't visit Bloom's mining sites without navigating the politics of who controls what ruins.

<TouristTip>

- **Gang Territory**: Bloom is controlled by three major gangs. Check with Serpent's Rest Outpost or Ruin Station for current territorial disputes before visiting abandoned sites. What's safe today might not be tomorrow.
- **Atmosphere**: Bloom's atmosphere is breathable at low altitude but thin. Supplemental O2 is recommended for extended surface operations, especially at deeper mining levels.
- **Water**: Bloom has expanses of liquid saltwater in temperate zones, but fresh water is limited. The abandoned mining sites have no functioning water treatment infrastructure.
- **Photography**: The best light for documenting the sites is during Bloom's long days, when the frozen biomes reflect sunlight across the mining scars. The contrast between industrial decay and natural landscape is the primary reason to visit.
- **Respect the Dead**: Some of these sites were occupied when the abandonment happened. You may find personal effects, body remnants, or burial sites. Treat them with the same respect you'd show any grave.

</TouristTip>
