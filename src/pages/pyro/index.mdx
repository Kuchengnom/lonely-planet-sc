The innermost world is a threat. Molten rock, corrosive atmosphere, and volcanic activity so constant that the surface changes faster than cartographers can map it. Home to some of the most profitable — and most dangerous — mining operations in the frontier. Ancient lava flows have frozen into vast Glass Fields of razor-edged obsidian, and a ridge of volcanic spires — the Spine — cuts across the northern hemisphere like a giant's backbone. Pyro I's calmer sibling, but only just. Geologically restless and visually stunning — the light refracts through the obsidian in ways that shouldn't exist in nature, creating permanent false-sun horizons that confuse navigation systems and human judgment alike.

By Pyro standards, practically a resort. The atmosphere is thin enough to breathe at low altitude, and the Old Serpent Channel hosts the Garden — the system's closest thing to normal life. Serpent's Rest Outpost is the unofficial capital, where independent operators, refuelers, and traders gather between deep-space runs. The best hand-pressed coffee in Pyro is here, usually served alongside stories that get more elaborate with each retelling.

The system's middle child. Temperate highlands, arid lowlands, and the Salvage Yards: a sprawling patchwork of scrap, workshops, and black-market trading posts where you can build an entire ship if you have enough time, money, and tolerance for things that might explode. The yards are a testament to Pyro's fundamental truth: nothing goes to waste that someone else can use. The tech here is questionable, but the prices are unbeatable.

</AttractionEntry>

The system's cold, quiet terminus. Most ships stop here only because they have to. The Amber Lakes glow with frozen methane in the star's red light, creating permanent amber horizons that give the world its name. The Pole Station conducts research nobody else would do — the scientists there have been silent for months, which either means they're making breakthroughs or they've frozen to death. The Frozen City in the northern hemisphere raises questions nobody has answered yet.

The outer system's dominant presence — a gas giant wrapped in ammonia storm bands, its Great Scarlet Spot visible from Pyro V on a clear day. Gas mining platforms float in the upper atmosphere, their crews working six-month shifts extracting fuel and ammonia for stations throughout the frontier. A spectacular ring system surrounds the planet, a graveyard of captured moons that disintegrate over geological time. Everything about it will kill you if you get too close. From a distance, though, it's intolerably beautiful.

## Eating and Drinking

Pyro's dining scene can be summarised in one word: survival. This is not a system that invests in cuisine. The food you find here is engineered for caloric adequacy, not enjoyment — and yet there are a few outliers that prove the rule. When you've spent the morning dodging insurance-clauser freighters and the afternoon flying through volcanic ash, a decent meal stops being a luxury and becomes a form of rebellion.

What makes Pyro's food culture interesting is that it divides itself along a single fault line: Pyro III is the closest thing the system has to civilisation, and everything else is a negotiation with necessity. Cross that line and you enter a world where food is either scavenged, synthesised, or cooked by people who learned from watching television cooking shows through a viewport. The contrast is stark, and honestly, it's the most entertaining thing about Pyro's economy — not the ore, not the salvage, but the way people have convinced themselves that eating in a system designed to kill them is a personality trait.

### Serpent's Rest — Pyro III

By far the best food in the entire system, located on the one planet that might qualify as "habitable." Serpent's Rest Outpost at the Old Serpent Channel is Pyro's unofficial capital and its only outpost with a claim to civilisation.

- **The Garden Café** — The best hand-pressed coffee in Pyro is here, and that's not marketing; it's a fact the system's pilots have unanimously agreed upon. The café is small, unassuming, and serves coffee that is genuinely good enough to make you forget you're in a frontier zone. The food is basic — breakfast items, sandwiches, occasional soup — but it's made fresh, and the atmosphere is the closest Pyro gets to a diner. The pastries are a particular triumph, especially on days when the bakery's oven doesn't run on the same generator as the outpost's atmospheric scrubbers.
- **Serpent's Kitchen** — The outpost's primary dining venue. It's not gourmet, but it's hot, it's filling, and the portions are generous enough to justify the price. The regulars are a mix of miners, haulers, and the occasional tourist who stumbled in and never left. The walls are lined with things people have traded for a seat at the table. The stew on cold days is a particular favourite among miners — heavy on the protein, thick enough to stand a spoon in, and served in bowls that have outlasted their owners' ships.

<TouristTip>

- **Coffee is currency**: If you want someone to tell you something useful in Pyro, start with a cup from the Garden Café and don't rush it. The information you get over that coffee is worth more than the price of the beans.
- **Serpent's Kitchen dinner is best**: Lunch is adequate. Dinner, when the whole outpost has eaten and the conversation gets loud, is where the real Pyro experience happens.

</TouristTip>

### Ruin Station — Pyro's Main Hub

Ruin Station at Lagrange Point 1 has eating options that range from acceptable to occasionally pleasant. It is the system's most populous single structure, and that density has created a food ecosystem that is more sophisticated than you would expect from a station assembled from derelicts. The food here is reliable, the prices are fair, and the company is where you get the best information in the entire frontier.

- **Food Court** — Whatever the shift rotation has produced. It won't win awards, but it's warm, it's edible, and the seating faces the viewport, which means you can watch freighters come and go while you chew. That counts for something. The noodle station on the eastern side of the hall is consistently the best option — the broth is made from something that tastes like it was sourced from the outer system, and the noodles have a texture that suggests whoever makes them trained elsewhere before ending up here.
- **The Grand Market Bakery** — A small shop on the lower level of the Grand Market that sells bread, pastries, and sandwiches. The bread is remarkable — it is real bread, not the compressed synth-loaf that passes for bakery goods at most frontier stations. The owner, a woman known only as "Mama Rhea," bakes at 4am station time every day and sells out by noon. If you want the croissants, arrive early. The croissants are the reason people come to the Grand Market on mornings they don't need to.
- **The Old Hub Bar** — A bar-restaurant hybrid on Ruin Station's central deck. The food is better than the drinks, which is a rare combination at frontier establishments. They serve a fish dish on days when they have caught fish from the hydroponics bay — it is delicate, unexpectedly fresh, and a reminder of what food tasted like before you committed to a system with six planets and no beaches. The bar has a pool table that is slightly crooked and a jukebox that plays music from somewhere around 2040.
- **The Docks Diner** — A small eatery in the docking ring section of the station, aimed at pilots who have just landed and haven't had a hot meal in hours. The food is American diner-style in the cheapest sense of the word: burgers, fries, milkshakes, and the kind of coffee that tastes like it was brewed in a ship's engine room. It is exactly what a pilot needs at 3am after a long jump. The milkshakes are the one item that genuinely surprises people — sweet, thick, and served in glasses that have never seen a dishwasher.

### Pyro IV — The Salvage Yards

The Salvage Yards are where you go if you want to see what food looks like when it is improvised. There are kitchens run by independent operators who have built entire supply chains out of scrap and ingenuity. The food here is rough, sometimes dangerous, occasionally brilliant.

- **Scrap & Spice** — A kitchen that should not exist, run by someone who treats food preparation like a salvage operation: nothing goes to waste, every ingredient is sourced through channels that would make a UEE compliance officer faint. The dishes are unpredictable. Some of them are excellent. Most of them are memorable. The name is not a joke — the menu changes daily based on what the operator has found or traded for. Arrive hungry. Leave confused but satisfied.
- **The Foundry Grub** — A canteen-style operation inside one of the larger Salvage Yards hangars. The food is industrial — maximum calories, minimum fuss — but the owner has developed a reputation for making a curry that is the real deal, cooked with spices that nobody on Pyro should have access to and somehow does. The seating is metal benches bolted to the hangar floor. The food is served on plastic trays. But the curry, on a planet where you haven't had a proper meal since you left orbit, will make you close your eyes and stand there for the full two minutes it takes to eat a bowl.

### Pyro I — The Mining Mess Halls

Pyro I is a mining world in the purest sense — every structure on the surface is a tool designed to extract something from a planet that would prefer you not be there. The dining reflects this utilitarian philosophy, with one exception: the mining companies have learned that fed workers are more productive workers, and the mess halls reflect a corporate calculus that sits somewhere between generosity and self-interest.

- **Helios Mining Cafeteria** — The company cafeteria for one of Pyro I's largest operations. The food is corporate standard: warm, filling, and completely devoid of the idea that eating might be enjoyable. The staff who work here develop a strange loyalty to the place over time — not because the food is good, but because it is the only heated building on the surface for fifty kilometres, and the company provides coffee that is genuinely decent. The coffee is the reason the cafeteria survives. Without it, the cafeteria would be a footnote.

### Pyro V — The Frozen End

The outermost planet is a research outpost disguised as a frontier settlement. The climate is brutal, the population is transient, and the food options are limited to what can be shipped through a quantum gate without freezing solid. The people who live here have adapted in the only way people always do: by building a food culture around what the cold permits.

- **Pole Station Mess Hall** — A research station dining facility that occasionally accepts outsiders. The food is scientific in the way the mess halls at the bottom of the ocean are scientific: it has been optimised for nutritional completeness and taste has been deprioritised to a degree that would be admirable if it were not so unnecessary. The meals are served on a schedule that the researchers follow religiously — breakfast, lunch, dinner, and a midnight snack that nobody admits they eat. The midnight snack is the best meal of the day.
- **Amber Lakes Café** — A small café on the edge of the frozen methane lakes. It is the kind of place that exists because someone wanted to make a living and the café happens to be the thing they are good at. The coffee is excellent, the pastries are imported from Pyro III (meaning they arrive stale but acceptable), and the view of the amber lakes in red starlight is something that nobody who has seen it will ever forget. Come here for the coffee and stay for the silence.

<TouristTip>
  
- **Ruin Station First**: Always dock at Ruin Station on arrival. It's neutral ground, has fuel and repairs, and the traders there will tell you what's actually happening in the system. Their intelligence is worth more than any cargo you're carrying.

- **Travel Advisory**: The UEE designates Pyro a frontier zone with limited presence. Insurance claims may be denied depending on cause. Comprehensive weapons coverage and a reliable escape plan are strongly recommended. Assume every ship you encounter is armed until proven otherwise.

- **No Lone Flying**: Single-ship travel in Pyro is statistically poor judgement. Travel with a wingman or stay near established traffic corridors. Even then — keep one eye on your sixth.

- **Credits vs. Cargo**: In Pyro, cargo sometimes speaks louder than UEC. Fuel cells, medical supplies, and food all trade at premium rates because getting them here is a risk premium. Arrive with something useful, leave with your life, and call it a good deal.

- **Navigation Hazards**: The Pyro Belt is a navigation nightmare. Magnetic interference from planetary formations disrupts sensors, and shipping lanes change based on which cartographer you trust. Download fresh routes at every stop and assume they're already wrong.

- **Law Enforcement**: There isn't any. Any dispute resolution is self-service, typically involving weapons. The only rules are don't start fights you can't finish and don't double-cross someone who knows where you live.

</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

Pyro is not one world — it is six, each with a different personality and a different way of killing you. The single most important lesson for any Pyro visitor: **plan your gear for the destination on your itinerary**. Gear that saves you on Pyro III will melt on Pyro I and freeze solid on Pyro V. The table below is your cheat sheet; the individual planet pages have the full breakdowns.

| Planet | Primary Threat | Must-Have Gear |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| **Pyro I** | Molten surface, corrosive atmosphere | Thermal mining suit (-1200°C), chemical scrubber, emergency beacon |
| **Pyro II** | Seismic activity, glass fields, thin toxic air | Thermal EVA suit (200°C+), reinforced boots, seismic detector |
| **Pyro III** | Thin breathable air, abrasive dust storms | Filter mask (N95+), supplemental O₂, wind-resistant outer layer |
| **Pyro IV** | Mud Flats (crust over thick mud), iron-oxide dust | Surface probe, ship dust guards, wind-resistant jacket |
| **Pyro V** | Extreme cold (-150°C+), frozen methane lakes | Thermal survival suit, heated liner, ice axe, anti-fog goggles |
| **Pyro VI** | Radiation, ring debris, gravity well | Ship radiation shielding, thruster fuel reserve, debris detection |
|| **Ruin Station** | Nothing — it's a station | Respect for the locals, barter goods, situational awareness |
