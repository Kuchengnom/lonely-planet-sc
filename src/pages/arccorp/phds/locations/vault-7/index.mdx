Vault 7 is ArcCorp's most secure storage facility, and possibly its most important. Located deep in the underground beneath Area18's eastern industrial sector, the vault holds the company's most valuable assets — rare materials, proprietary technology, classified data, and the kind of secrets that could bring down governments if they were made public. What is actually inside Vault 7 is unknown. ArcCorp's public records describe it as a "strategic materials reserve," but the company's own leaks, defectors, and the whispered conversations of pilots who dock at Baijini Point paint a much more complicated picture.

Vault 7 does not appear on any official map. ArcCorp's public infrastructure charts make no reference to it, and the company's internal documents (those that have leaked) describe it with a level of classification that suggests the only people who know its full contents are the three people who designed it, and one of them is dead.

The mystery of Vault 7 is part of what makes ArcCorp compelling. Every industry insider knows it exists. No one knows what's inside. And the gap between those two facts has generated more speculation, more conspiracy theories, and more career-ending curiosity than any other single location in human space.

## Getting There

Vault 7 is not accessible to visitors. This is not a limitation of the guide — it is a physical reality enforced by ArcCorp security, structural barriers, and a level of classified clearance that is not available to anyone outside the company's innermost circles. The following information describes Vault 7's location and security layout based on community triangulation, leaked documents, and the accounts of people who have been inside (or close enough to know something is there). This information should be treated as background knowledge, not navigation advice. If you attempt to find Vault 7, you will not succeed. And if you do, you will not leave.

**From the Underground Market Passage:**

The nearest publicly accessible location to Vault 7 is the Market Passage, approximately 800 meters east of the Primary SynFood Lift. From the Market Passage, the vault is estimated to be approximately 200–300 meters below, in a direction that corresponds roughly to the eastern industrial sector's vertical axis. The passages connecting the Market Passage to the vault area are not publicly accessible — they are sealed, locked, and monitored.

**Security Layers:**

Community reports and leaked documents describe at least three distinct security layers between the Market Passage and Vault 7:

1. **Surface-level screening:** Any descent from the surface to the Underground is monitored by ArcCorp security cameras and personnel. The cameras are not covert — they are clearly visible, and they are the first layer of deterrence.

2. **Underground patrol routes:** ArcCorp security maintains regular patrol routes through the primary Underground passages. These routes are not on a fixed schedule, and they converge on the areas surrounding Vault 7 with increasing frequency as you get closer.

3. **Vault perimeter:** The area immediately surrounding Vault 7 is heavily fortified. Multiple security checkpoints, biometric scanners, and physical barriers (including blast doors and reinforced concrete) create a perimeter that has never been breached by unauthorized personnel.

**Access Attempts:**

There are three documented (and unsuccessful) attempts to access Vault 7:

- **2947:** A group of ArcCorp contractors attempted to bypass the perimeter using a decommissioned maintenance shaft. They were detected by motion sensors before reaching the vault perimeter and were detained without incident.
- **2951:** An independent data broker claimed to have accessed Vault 7's exterior and photographed the blast doors. The photographs were never verified, and the broker has not been seen or heard from since.
- **2954:** A former ArcCorp security employee claimed to have worked on the vault's design and provided detailed (but unverified) descriptions of its interior layout. The employee disappeared two weeks after providing the information.

## What Is Inside

What is inside Vault 7 is the subject of extensive speculation and zero verified evidence. The following theories are based on community reports, leaked documents, and informed guessing. None of them have been confirmed. All of them are interesting.

**The Strategic Materials Theory:** ArcCorp's official explanation is that Vault 7 stores strategic materials — rare minerals, proprietary alloys, and experimental compounds that are essential to the company's industrial operations. This is plausible, and it is consistent with the vault's construction. The vault's walls are reinforced with materials that are not commercially available, and its interior climate control systems are rated for conditions that no surface facility would require.

**The Classified Data Theory:** Vault 7 may store ArcCorp's most sensitive data — classified research, legal documents, and the kind of information that could be devastating if released. The vault's interior includes what appear to be server rooms, and the company has a long history of using underground facilities for data storage in case of surface-level threats. This theory is supported by the fact that ArcCorp's public data centers are distributed across multiple planets, making a centralized underground backup plausible.

**The Experimental Technology Theory:** Some industry insiders believe that Vault 7 stores experimental ArcCorp technologies — prototypes, weapons systems, and research projects that are too advanced or too controversial for public view. This theory is less substantiated but consistent with ArcCorp's pattern of developing technologies in secret and releasing them only when they are ready to be monetized.

**The Human Element:** The most controversial theory is that Vault 7 does not store materials or data — it stores people. ArcCorp has a long history of detaining individuals for reasons that are not publicly disclosed, and the vault's security measures are consistent with a high-security detention facility as much as they are with a storage facility. There is no evidence to support this theory. There is also no evidence to definitively refute it.

## The History of Vault 7

Vault 7 was constructed in the early days of ArcCorp's colonization, during a period when the company was building the infrastructure that would become Area18. The original plans for the facility were classified, and the construction was carried out by contractors who were not told what they were building. The workers who constructed Vault 7 were never asked to describe what they had built — they were paid, they were paid well, and they were told to forget everything they had seen.

The vault was operational within two years of construction beginning, and it has been in continuous use since. Its contents have changed over time — some materials were added, some were removed, and some have remained untouched for decades. The vault's security systems have been upgraded multiple times, and each upgrade has made it more difficult for unauthorized personnel to access.

ArcCorp has never publicly discussed Vault 7. The company's only statement on the facility was a brief mention in a 2945 press release: "Vault 7 is a strategic materials reserve that supports ArcCorp's industrial operations. Its existence is not a secret, but its contents are proprietary." This statement was immediately followed by a non-disclosure agreement that the reporter signed before the press conference began.

## Safety Considerations

- **Legal risk:** Attempting to access Vault 7 is a violation of multiple ArcCorp security protocols and potentially UEE law. The penalties include fines, imprisonment, and permanent banishment from ArcCorp space. The actual penalties are likely more severe, but they are not publicly disclosed.
- **Physical risk:** The areas surrounding Vault 7 are actively monitored by ArcCorp security. If you are detected, you will be detained. If you resist, you will be subdued. If you attempt to flee, you will be pursued. The security forces are armed, trained, and authorized to use lethal force.
- **Professional risk:** Any attempt to access Vault 7 will be recorded. The footage will be reviewed by ArcCorp's legal department, and any connection to the attempt will be noted in your employment and travel records. You may find that no one in ArcCorp space will hire you, dock you, or serve you after an attempt.
- **Personal risk:** The people who work at ArcCorp know who you are, where you have been, and what you have tried to do. If you attempt to access Vault 7, you should expect to be watched. You should expect to be followed. And you should expect that the next time you arrive on ArcCorp, you will be met by security before you even leave your ship.
