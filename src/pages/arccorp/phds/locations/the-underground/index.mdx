The Underground is not on any map. It does not appear on official ArcCorp infrastructure charts, and the company's public records do not acknowledge its existence. It is a vast subterranean network of tunnels, chambers, and passageways that stretches beneath most of Area18 and the surrounding industrial zones, extending for hundreds of kilometers in every direction. What began as emergency shelters and backup manufacturing facilities during ArcCorp's earliest days has evolved into something entirely unexpected — a living, breathing city beneath the city, with its own economy, social structures, and power dynamics.

The Underground is where ArcCorp goes when it doesn't want to be seen. It is the city's nervous system, its secret economy, and occasionally, its conscience. Some of the best food on ArcCorp is made here. Some of the worst crimes are committed here. Most of the people who live here have chosen this life, and none of them plan to leave.

## Getting There

The Underground has no official entrances. All access points are unofficial, unofficially tolerated, and unofficially subject to being sealed without warning by ArcCorp security. The passages that follow describe known access points as of the most recent community reports — but these are subject to change without notice. If you arrive and the entrance is sealed, that means it was sealed. Do not attempt to force your way in.

**Primary Entrance — SynFood Lift, Area18 Lower Tier 3:**

The most commonly used entrance is located behind the SynFood Kiosk in Sector D of Area18's Lower Tier 3. Behind the kiosk's rear storage room, there is a maintenance lift that has been out of service for approximately twenty years. The lift's control panel has been bypassed by local operators, and pulling the emergency release handle on the left side of the panel (marked with a piece of red tape that says "EMERGENCY — DO NOT PULL" in handwriting that predates ArcCorp's rebranding) will disengage the safety lock and allow the lift to descend to the Underground level. The drop is approximately 85 meters. The lift has no floor indicators, and the doors open directly into a well-lit main passage with signage in no language you'll recognize on the first visit.

**Secondary Entrance — Refinery District Cargo Shaft:**

Located in the lower reaches of the Refinery District, this entrance is accessible through the rear loading bay of a decommissioned processing plant. The shaft itself has been used for cargo transport for decades and has been repurposed as a vertical access route. It requires a functional climb or a makeshift rope system, and the edges are slick with condensation and chemical residue. This entrance is accessible during daylight hours only and requires a visitor pass from the Area18 Information Desk on the surface. The pass costs approximately 15 UEC and is supposed to be checked at the entrance. The people who live in the Underground will ask if you have it. You do not need to show it. They will ask again anyway.

**Tertiary Entrance — Tram Tunnel Beneath the Observatory:**

The least known and least trafficked entrance is accessible from the basement level of the Area18 Observatory. A service door behind the maintenance room leads to an old tram tunnel that was decommissioned when the modern tram system was built. The tunnel is poorly lit and narrow, and it connects directly to a well-maintained passage on the Underground level. This entrance is the quietest and the safest, but also the least useful for reaching the more interesting parts of the Underground — it deposits you in a residential sector that is more dormitory than destination.

<TouristTip>
  The SynFood lift is the most reliable entrance, but it is also the most heavily monitored. ArcCorp security patrols the Upper Tier 3 regularly, and while they do not have a permanent presence in the lift itself, they check its maintenance logs and will close it if they find tampering. The best time to use the lift is during shift change (approximately 06:00 and 18:00 Area18 time), when the lower levels are crowded and distracted. The Refinery District entrance requires no timing but demands physical capability. The Observatory entrance is safest but least interesting. Pick the one that matches your situation.
</TouristTip>

## What You'll Find

The Underground is divided into several distinct sectors, each with its own character and reputation. The sectors are not formally organized — there is no government, no administration, no official map — but the people who live there have developed an informal sense of territory that most visitors learn quickly or regret not learning quickly.

**The Market Passage:** The heart of the Underground's economy, the Market Passage is a wide, well-lit tunnel that stretches approximately two kilometers from the SynFood lift to the far eastern residential blocks. It is lined with stalls, kiosks, and small shops run by people who have been operating here for as long as anyone can remember. The goods range from the mundane (food, clothing, basic tools) to the exotic (smuggled electronics, contraband data drives, artifacts from abandoned surface facilities). Prices are negotiable but not deeply so — the people running the stalls know what things are worth, and they have been doing this long enough to be very good at pricing. The food in the Market Passage is some of the best on ArcCorp. Small kitchens in the rear stalls produce noodle soups, grilled proteins, and bread that is actually made from wheat, all of which are remarkable achievements on a planet that cannot grow a single crop naturally.

**The Residential Blocks:** The Eastern and Western Blocks are large clusters of rooms and small apartments built into chambers that were originally designed for industrial storage. Families live here, sometimes for generations. The rooms are small, the walls are thin, and the noise is constant. But the sense of community is strong, and the people are surprisingly hospitable to newcomers who show respect and ask permission before taking photographs or exploring off-limited areas. There is no formal leadership in the Residential Blocks, but certain families have served as informal mediators for disputes for decades. Approach them first if you have a problem that cannot be solved with money.

**The Research Labs:** ArcCorp contractors maintain several research facilities in the Underground, working on projects that do not appear on any corporate schedule. These labs are staffed by scientists, engineers, and technicians who have either been assigned to work underground or have chosen to disappear into the Underground's anonymity. The nature of the research is classified, and the doors are locked. But the people who work there are visible in the Market Passage, and they are sometimes willing to talk — carefully, quietly, and only if they trust you not to repeat what they say. The general consensus among Underground residents is that the labs are where ArcCorp does the work it does not want the UEE to know about. This is almost certainly true.

**The Old Tunnels:** Beyond the well-maintained passages lie the Old Tunnels — abandoned sections of the Underground that were sealed off when more efficient routes were built. They are dark, poorly supported, and often flooded. The air is stale and the structural integrity is questionable. But they contain some of the most interesting remnants of the Underground's history: old machinery, faded graffiti from previous generations of residents, and occasionally, things that have been left behind by ArcCorp security during expeditions that were not meant to be found. Exploring the Old Tunnels is not recommended for first-time visitors. If you go, go with someone who has been here before. Bring a light. Bring an axe. Bring a buddy.

**The Black Market:** There is no formal black market in the Underground — that would imply organization, and the Underground's economy is fundamentally informal. But there are traders who sell things that are not meant to be sold, in places that are not meant to be found. The black market operates in the rear of the Market Passage, in side alleys that are poorly lit and not often frequented. If you are looking for something specific, you need to ask for it — and you need to know someone who knows someone who knows someone. The prices are high, the risks are higher, and the things you can buy are the kind of things that make you think twice about what "price" means.

## Photographic Opportunities

The Underground offers some of the most compelling photography on ArcCorp, precisely because it is uncurated. There are no scenic overlooks or designated viewpoints. Every shot has to be earned with a descent, a negotiation with locals, and the willingness to stand in places that are not designed for standing.

**Market Passage at Night:** The Market Passage takes on a completely different character after dark — and by "after dark," I mean after the surface shifts change and the upper levels empty out. The stall lights create long shadows down the tunnel, the steam from the noodle stalls rises in slow motion, and the people who are still around have the kind of tired, focused energy that makes for incredible portraits. Bring a fast lens and a tripod. The ambient light is low but not absent.

**Residential Block Life:** The Eastern and Western Blocks offer intimate glimpses into Underground life — children playing in narrow corridors, elders sitting outside their doors, families cooking in small communal kitchens. These are not staged moments. They are real life, and they are the most authentic thing you will see on ArcCorp. Ask permission before photographing people. Most will say yes. Some will not. Respect the ones who say no.

**Old Tunnel Exploration:** The abandoned sections of the Underground are visually striking — rusted machinery, water damage, decades of decay preserved in the cool, dry air. The graffiti alone is worth the trip. But the Old Tunnels are dangerous, and the most dramatic shots are also the ones taken at the greatest risk. Do not go alone. Do not go without proper lighting. And do not go if you are not prepared for the structural instability.

## Safety Considerations

- **Air quality:** The Underground's ventilation is poor. Air quality varies by sector but is generally worse than the already-poor surface air. A heavy-duty filter mask is essential, especially in the Market Passage where cooking fumes, industrial residue, and human occupancy combine to create an atmosphere that will trigger your cough reflex within minutes.
- **Structural integrity:** Many passages, especially in the Old Tunnels, are not structurally sound. Do not enter areas with visible cracks in the ceiling, sagging supports, or water damage. The Underground was designed for emergency shelter, not for tourism. If something looks like it might fall, it probably will.
- **ArcCorp security:** Security patrols are irregular but present. They do not have a standing order to arrest visitors, but they do have a standing order to close any entrance they find being used. If you encounter security, cooperate fully and exit calmly. Do not argue. Do not resist. Do not become an incident report.
- **Personal safety:** The Underground is not dangerous in the way that violence-adjacent places are. It is dangerous in the way that poverty-adjacent places are — malnutrition, poor health, lack of medical care, and desperation. Most people are not hostile, but they are hard, and they have survived by developing a very low tolerance for people who treat them like tourists. Be respectful. Ask before photographing people. Buy your food from the stalls. Tip generously.
- **Navigation:** The Underground is not navigable without local knowledge. The passages branch, loop, and terminate in dead ends. Maps are unreliable — they were drawn by people who knew where they were going, and the Underground changes faster than anyone can update a paper document. If you lose your way, find the nearest market stall, ask for directions, and buy something while you wait for the answer.

<TouristTip>
  The single most important rule of visiting the Underground: do not photograph ArcCorp security, contractors, or their facilities. This is not a suggestion. This is the rule that will get you in the most trouble. ArcCorp security does not take kindly to being photographed, and the contractors are even less tolerant. If you accidentally photograph a security detail or a research lab entrance, delete the image immediately and do not mention it. If security asks you to delete it, delete it immediately and do not argue. The people who run the market stalls can help you if you get into trouble — but only if they think you are a respectful visitor and not a spy.
</TouristTip>
