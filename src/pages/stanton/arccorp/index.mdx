<AttractionEntry number={1} title="Area18 — The Last Open Space" category="settlement" image="/images/arccorp/area18-aerial.jpeg" imageAlt="Aerial view of Area18's neon-lit sprawl">

Area18 is ArcCorp's main commercial port — which sounds grand until you realize it is one of the only places on the planet where you can still see the sky. The landing zone is a tangle of industrial scars, hab blocks, and the occasional attempt at greenery that lasted until the acid rain killed it. But it is alive. The streets are crowded with workers, traders, and the occasional tourist who thought "most industrialized planet" would be interesting in a fun way.

The ArcCorp Tower dominates everything. It is the corporate headquarters, the tallest structure on a planet that measures success in stories, and a monument to the company that owns the world. The surrounding districts are a mix of factory housing, commercial zones, and the kind of bars that don't ask questions about your employment history. The Observatory is worth the climb — not for the view, which is mostly more city, but for the scale of the thing. You stand there and try to comprehend that every light you can see is another factory, another home, another life owned by the same corporation.

The food stalls on the lower levels are where the real city lives. Synthetic-protein noodles, nutrient paste "burgers," and drinks that taste like regret but cost like ambition. It is not good. It is compelling.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="Cubby Blast" category="hidden-find" image="/images/arccorp/cubby-blast.jpeg" imageAlt="The famous Cubby Blast storefront in Area18">

Every city has a shop that becomes more than a shop. Cubby Blast is Area18's. Founded by Clor Vee in a back-alley stall of the fourth tier, it grew through the Avasi Scandal of 2931 into a storefront with a favorable lease and a reputation. Vee himself is a character — sharp-eyed, quiet, and supposedly in possession of a memory that never forgets a face or a debt. The shop sells personal firearms and ship weapons, the kind of thing a pilot needs when they are hauling goods through unpatrolled space.

But the real reason to visit is the atmosphere. Vee's client list includes everyone from corporate security to the kind of freelancers who prefer not to file paperwork. The walls are covered in thank-you notes from customers who claim the gear saved their life, next to news clippings of the ones who didn't make it. The contrast is the point. This is ArcCorp in miniature: opportunity and risk, side by side, with no one pretending either is safe.

<TouristTip>
  Don't try to haggle. Vee's prices are fixed, and his tolerance for negotiation is famously low. If you are looking for something specific, ask directly. He either has it or knows where to get it. The back-alley stalls of the fourth tier are where the real deals happen, but they are also where the real problems start.
</TouristTip>

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={3} title="The ArcCorp Suburbs" category="urban-sight" image="/images/arccorp/arccorp-suburbs.jpeg" imageAlt="The endless industrial suburbs of ArcCorp">

The suburbs of ArcCorp are not suburban in any recognizable sense. They are the spaces between the major industrial zones, filled with the residential blocks and local supplies that keep the factory workers housed and fed. The view from any window is another building. The streets are narrow, the air is thick with industrial residue, and the only green is the occasional company-planted tree that struggles to survive the acid rain.

But there is a strange beauty to it. The neon signs reflect off rain-slicked surfaces in colours that no one planned. The sounds of a thousand factories merge into something like music if you have the right perspective. And at night, the whole planet glows, a continuous carpet of light that has no beginning and no end.

The fusion plants that ring the equator are visible from orbit — a necklace of blue-white energy that powers the world. Tourists are invited to take factory tours, though the actual industrial facilities are too dangerous for visitors. The sanitized versions are complete with holograms and animatronics. The real thing is best viewed from a distance.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={4} title="Lyria — The Cryovolcanic Moon" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/arccorp/lyria-cryovolcanoes.jpeg" imageAlt="Active cryovolcanoes on the surface of Lyria" href="/lonely-planet-sc/stanton/arccorp/lyria">

Lyria is ArcCorp's closest moon, and it is the exact opposite of the planet it orbits. Where ArcCorp is hot, crowded, and entirely artificial, Lyria is cold, empty, and violently natural. The surface is covered in active cryogeysers and cryovolcanoes — eruptions of supercooled methane and nitrogen that freeze on contact with the surface and build into bizarre, transient sculptures. Nothing here is permanent. The landscape changes every time the volcanoes wake up.

The moon has no permanent settlement, which makes it one of the few places in the Stanton system where you can stand and hear nothing but your own breathing. The eruptions are unpredictable but generally safe to observe from a distance — the real danger is the cold, which will overwhelm most suit heaters in minutes. Bring a thermal upgrade. The silence is worth it.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={5} title="The Refinery Districts" category="practical" image="/images/arccorp/refinery-district.jpeg" imageAlt="The glowing refinery districts of ArcCorp at night">

The refinery districts are ArcCorp's beating heart and its most dangerous neighborhoods. Towering processing plants convert raw ore into usable materials 24 hours a day, their exhaust stacks pumping pollutants into an atmosphere that was already struggling. The workers who live here rarely see daylight — their shifts are long, the pay is adequate, and the turnover is constant.

Tourists don't usually come here. If they did, they would find some of the best food on the planet — small kitchens run by families who have been feeding refinery workers for generations, serving dishes that can only exist here: noodles in synthetic broth, protein blocks grilled until they taste like meat, and drinks that make you forget what the air smells like. The rougher the district, the better the food. That is a universal rule, and ArcCorp is no exception.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={6} title="Baijini Point" category="practical" image="/images/arccorp/baijini-point.jpeg" imageAlt="Baijini Point station orbiting above ArcCorp">

Baijini Point is the orbital station above Area18, and in many ways it is the better way to experience the planet. The station has a cargo deck, ship services, and a respectable food court, but the real attraction is the permanent observation deck. From here, ArcCorp looks like a single organism — a vast, glowing entity that breathes smoke and light and never sleeps.

The station's bar is a favourite of pilots who have seen too much of Area18's surface and prefer their industry at a distance. The drinks are expensive, the view is free, and the conversation is exactly what you would expect from people who spend their lives ferrying goods to a planet that only stops growing when it runs out of space.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={7} title="Wala — The Prolate Moon" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/arccorp/wala-orbit.jpeg" imageAlt="The prolate shape of Wala as seen from orbit" href="/lonely-planet-sc/stanton/arccorp/wala">

Wala is ArcCorp's second moon, and it is visibly strange. The low density of the moon makes it particularly susceptible to tidal forces, and the result is a noticeably prolate shape — it is wider than it is tall, a squashed ball of rock and ice that looks like it was squeezed by an invisible hand. The surface is unremarkable: cratered, cold, and empty. But the view of ArcCorp from Wala's surface is one of the best in the system. The planet fills the sky, a carpet of lights and storms that stretch from horizon to horizon, and you realize that everything you are looking at is city. Every light is a person. Every building is a life. And somewhere in there, someone is looking up at you.

## Eating and Drinking

ArcCorp's food scene is an argument about what "food" means when you can manufacture anything but the desire to sit down and enjoy it. The planet produces a startling amount of edible matter — synthetic proteins, processed grains, lab-grown vegetables — but turning that into a meal worth remembering is a skill that exists entirely in the informal economy. The official venues are what you'd expect: corporate cafeterias designed to keep workers fed and mobile, and food courts where you can buy calories in paper wrappers while standing in a hallway. The good stuff is underground. Literally. The lower levels of Area18, the kitchens attached to the refinery complexes, and the small bars between the corporate towers are where people on ArcCorp actually eat — and where the city's food culture has survived despite everything working against it.

### Area18 — The Lower Food Stalls

The food stalls on Area18's lower levels are the closest thing the planet has to a dining scene. They are not elegant. They are not clean by any standard that doesn't involve industrial safety violations. But they are the heart of ArcCorp's food culture, and they are worth the descent.

- **SynFood Kiosk (Lower Tier 3)** — The most reliable place for a quick bite in Area18. SynFood is ArcCorp's answer to fast food: branded, standardized, and somehow not terrible. The synthetic chicken noodle soup is the best thing they serve, and it arrives hot in a bowl that will probably be the only thing keeping your hands warm for the rest of the night. It costs about as much as a cup of coffee on Earth used to cost. The name is a joke nobody tells anymore.
- **The Noodle Cart at Level 2B** — Run by a woman who has been running it since before the Avasi Scandal cleaned up the lower tiers. The noodles are hand-pulled, the broth is synthetic but well-seasoned, and the chili oil — which she imports from somewhere that actually grows chili peppers — is worth the entire trip. You find her by following the steam through the lower levels. She doesn't have a sign. She doesn't need one.
- **Protein Block Grill** — A stall that does exactly what it says. Thick slabs of textured protein, grilled with whatever fat and spice are available that day, served on flatbread that is better than it should be on a planet that doesn't grow wheat. The workers from the nearby factories come here at shift change, and the line is long because everyone agrees this is the best protein in Area18. It might be. It's certainly the most honest.

### The Refinery District Kitchens

The kitchens attached to the refinery districts are where the food culture of ArcCorp was born, and they are still the best food on the planet. They are not restaurants in any conventional sense. They are family-run kitchens that have been feeding refinery workers for generations, and the dishes on their menus exist only because someone's grandmother figured out how to make something edible out of industrial protein and hope.

- **The Chen Family Kitchen** — Located in the lower reaches of the refinery district, near the processing plants that give the area its name. The Chens have been cooking here since the 2920s, and their reputation is built on one dish: a noodle dish in synthetic broth with protein blocks, grilled vegetables that may or may not be vegetables, and a sauce that is the family secret. The kitchen has twelve seats. The food costs roughly what a single day's wages should cover. The wait is worth it.
- **Reclamation & Disposal Canteen** — Not a restaurant, not really. It's a canteen attached to a waste processing facility, and the food exists because the company knows that fed workers are less likely to complain about the conditions. The food is hot, it is substantial, and it tastes like it was made by someone who has seen worse and learned to cook anyway. Don't ask where the protein comes from. The workers who eat here have answered that question with a look and moved on.

### Baijini Point — The Orbital Food Court

Baijini Point is the orbital station above Area18, and in many ways it is the better way to experience the planet. The station has a cargo deck, ship services, and a respectable food court, but the real attraction is the permanent observation deck. From here, ArcCorp looks like a single organism — a vast, glowing entity that breathes smoke and light and never sleeps.

- **The Food Court** — The station's food court is the kind of place that exists because the station needs to feed the people who work there, but it's better than you'd expect. There's a noodle bar, a protein wrap counter, and a dessert station that serves something called "area cream" which is sweet, cold, and entirely synthetic in a way that makes you grateful for the invention of sugar. The seating overlooks the observation windows. You eat while ArcCorp glows below you. It is a strange and appropriate thing.
- **The Station Bar** — The station's bar is a favourite of pilots who have seen too much of Area18's surface and prefer their industry at a distance. The drinks are expensive, the view is free, and the conversation is exactly what you would expect from people who spend their lives ferrying goods to a planet that only stops growing when it runs out of space. The bar snacks are terrible. The view is not.

### Corporate Cafeterias — Factory Line

ArcCorp's megacorporations have invested in feeding their employees, and the result is a network of corporate cafeterias that are efficient, warm, and largely forgettable. The Factory Line outlets scattered across Area18 have attached cafeterias that serve the kind of food that keeps people productive and rarely makes them think about anything beyond the next shift. The coffee is decent. The seating is warm. It is the kind of place where you come when you are hungry, you eat, and you leave. Nothing about it is memorable. Everything about it is functional. And sometimes, on a planet where the air smells like sulfur and the rain eats paint, functional is exactly what you need.

<TouristTip>
  The lower levels of Area18 can be disorienting for first-time visitors. The food stalls are on Tier 2B and below — follow the smell of cooking oil and synthetic broth. The refinery district kitchens are accessible only during daylight hours and require a visitor pass from the Area18 information desk. The food court on Baijini Point is open to all visitors and is the only dining option on the station that doesn't require you to be a crew member or have a ship docked. Budget 30–100 UEC for a meal in the lower levels; 150–300 UEC on Baijini Point. The SynFood kiosk is the most affordable option at around 20 UEC per bowl.
</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

ArcCorp's atmosphere is technically breathable but hostile — warm, thick with industrial particulates, and regularly drenched in acid rain. The key challenge is not survival so much as comfort: the air will not kill you in minutes, but it will ruin your equipment, irritate your lungs, and make every outdoor experience a negotiation with the atmosphere.

<ExplorerView slot="explorer" author="Freight Pilot, ArcCorp Docks">
ArcCorp doesn't try to kill you. It just doesn't care if you die while it's building something. My first visit, I showed up in a standard EVA suit and spent six hours covered in grime that no amount of wiping would remove. Second time, I bought a filter mask, some goggles, and a coat that was basically a tarp. Still got dirty. But I came back alive and my lungs didn't sound like a broken compressor. That's a win.
</ExplorerView>

**Essential gear:**

- **Respiratory filter mask** — ArcCorp's air quality is classified as poor. The acid rain and industrial emissions make prolonged breathing without filtration uncomfortable and potentially harmful. A filter mask from Tammany & Sons in Area18 works; you can also bring your own. The lower levels of Area18 and the refinery districts demand the best filtration you can find.
- **Acid-resistant outer layer** — The rain on ArcCorp is corrosive. A standard jacket will degrade within a day of regular exposure. Acid-resistant material is essential for any outdoor work, and the synthetic fabrics sold in Area18's market are purpose-built for this.
- **Sealed footwear with chemical-resistant soles** — The lower levels of Area18 and the refinery districts have puddles of unknown composition. Standard boots will degrade. Chemical-resistant soles are the minimum; sealed boots are the recommendation.
- **Protective goggles with sealed edges** — Industrial particulates and acid rain both threaten the eyes. Sealed goggles prevent both particulates and rain from reaching the eyes. They also protect against the glare of neon reflections off wet surfaces, which is surprisingly disorienting in the lower levels.
- **Waterproof gear bag** — Electronics, documents, and spare clothing need protection from the acid rain. A sealed waterproof bag is essential. The rain comes without warning and with intensity that drenches everything in minutes.
- **UV-resistant sunscreen** — The ArcCorp atmosphere scatters ultraviolet light in unpredictable ways. Combined with the constant glare off wet surfaces and neon, UV exposure is a significant risk even indoors near windows.

**Nice-to-have extras:**

- **Portable air quality meter** — The air quality in different districts of ArcCorp varies dramatically. The lower levels of Area18 are worst; Baijini Point (orbital) is essentially pristine. A meter helps you choose where to spend time outdoors.
- **Multi-tool** — Useful in the lower levels where things break, leak, or need to be pried open. ArcCorp runs on improvisation, and a multi-tool goes with the philosophy.
- **LED headlamp** — The lower levels and refinery districts have very poor lighting. An LED headlamp illuminates the spaces that tourists rarely see and the ones that are most interesting.
- **Camera with weather sealing** — ArcCorp is one of the most photogenic planets in human space, but the acid rain and industrial particulates will destroy an unprotected camera. Weather-sealed equipment is essential.
- **Small first aid kit** — Industrial accidents are common. A basic first aid kit with bandages, antiseptic, and antihistamine pills covers most minor incidents.

<TouristTip>
  The acid rain on ArcCorp is not a joke. It can eat through standard fabric and corrode metal in prolonged exposure. If you are staying on the surface for more than a few hours, budget 50–100 UEC for a proper acid-resistant outfit from an outfitter in Area18. The cheapest option is the SynFood kiosk's rain poncho — it works for an hour or two but will disintegrate after repeated use.
</TouristTip>
