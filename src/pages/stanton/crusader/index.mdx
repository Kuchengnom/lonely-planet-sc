<AttractionEntry number={1} title="Orison — City in the Clouds" category="settlement" image="/images/crusader/orison-city.jpeg" imageAlt="The floating platforms of Orison against Crusader's cloud layer">

Orison shouldn't exist. A city built in the upper atmosphere of a gas giant, suspended by nothing more than engineering and optimism. Crusader Industries built it, piece by piece, platform by platform, until it became the most beautiful impossible thing in the Stanton system. The city is a lattice of interconnected platforms — residential, industrial, and commercial — all floating at the edge of the breathable layer, where the air is thin but the views are endless.

The Providence industrial platforms are where the real work happens. Massive transport vessels hang in the construction gantries, surrounded by a web of scaffolding and lights. The scale is impossible to describe. You stand on a viewing platform and watch a ship the size of a small town being assembled in open air, with nothing beneath it but clouds. It is, genuinely, one of the most remarkable things human engineering has ever produced. And it is beautiful in a way that makes you forget how fragile the whole thing is.

The residential platforms are quieter. Cloudrest Retreat and Empyrean Park offer something approaching luxury, if your definition of luxury includes the constant reminder that you are floating over an infinite drop. The sunsets are the best in the system — the light scatters through Crusader's upper atmosphere into colours that don't have names, and the whole city glows.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="The Shipyards of Crusader Industries" category="urban-sight" image="/images/crusader/shipyards.jpeg" imageAlt="Massive transport vessels under construction in the Crusader shipyards">

The Crusader Industries shipyards are where the Genesis Starliner and other massive commercial vessels are born. They are also, incidentally, one of the most visually stunning locations in the Stanton system. The shipyards are a web of platforms, gantries, and construction harnesses suspended in mid-air, lit from below by the ambient glow of Crusader's cloud layer and from above by floodlights that never sleep.

The best time to visit is during a shift change, when the platforms are busiest and the lights are most dramatic. But the best view is from a distance — fly a small craft to one of the outer observation platforms and watch the whole thing shimmer in the mist. It looks like a constellation that someone built by hand.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={3} title="Cellin" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/crusader/cellin-volcanoes.jpeg" imageAlt="Dormant volcanic landscape on Cellin" href="/lonely-planet-sc/stanton/crusader/cellin">

Named after the youngest of three siblings from a 24th-century children's tale, Cellin is as temperamental as its namesake. Over a hundred dormant volcanoes dot the surface, reminders of a planet that is technically asleep but clearly dreaming of fire. The terrain is rough, the geology is unstable, and the whole place feels like it could wake up at any moment. Crusader Industries maintains Security Post Kareah in orbit — supposedly for "research", but everyone knows it is there because the moon attracts exactly the kind of people who need watching.

The surface is a mix of hardened lava flows and ash plains, with the occasional hot spring where miners have set up temporary camps. The view of Crusader from Cellin's surface is worth the landing — the gas giant fills half the sky, its cloud bands swirling in colours that never repeat. Just watch your step. The crust is thin in places.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={4} title="Daymar" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/crusader/daymar-desert.jpeg" imageAlt="The vast desert of Daymar" href="/lonely-planet-sc/stanton/crusader/daymar">

Daymar is the largest of Crusader's moons and the one that feels most like a place someone might actually want to visit. It is a desert world — not the hot, dry kind, but the cold, atmospheric kind where the dust gets into everything and the sunsets last for hours. The terrain is a mix of canyons, mesas, and vast open flats that look like they belong on a completely different planet. Which, technically, they do.

The surface is littered with the remains of failed mining operations and the occasional outpost that was abandoned when the money ran out. Covalex Hub Gundo was one of the more ambitious attempts — a logistics platform in orbit that someone decided was worth sabotaging. The wreckage is still up there, a reminder that corporate ambition doesn't always end well. On the surface, the real attraction is the silence. Daymar is empty in a way that few places in the Stanton system are. You can walk for hours without seeing another living thing. The dust swirls around your boots and the only sound is the wind.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={5} title="Yela" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/crusader/yela-ice.jpeg" imageAlt="The frozen surface of Yela with Crusader in the sky" href="/lonely-planet-sc/stanton/crusader/yela">

Yela is the oldest of Crusader's three moons, and it shows. The surface is a water-ice crust that has been there since before anyone named it, pockmarked with craters and split by stress fractures from millions of years of tidal squeezing. It is, in its way, beautiful — a frozen world under a frozen sky, with the gas giant hanging overhead like a vast, glowing wall.

The real attraction is GrimHEX. The Nine Tails — a pirate group with more ambition than sense — carved a landing zone into one of Yela's larger asteroids and have been squatting there ever since. The station is a monument to improvised engineering, held together with welding, hope, and the occasional threat of violence. The locals are what you would expect: opportunists, smugglers, and the occasional honest trader who just wants somewhere to refuel without answering questions. The bar is terrible. The stories are worse. But there is nowhere else in the system quite like it.

<TouristTip>
  GrimHEX is not a place for tourists who want to feel safe. But it is the only place in the Stanton system where you can drink with pirates while watching a gas giant rise over an asteroid field. Bring a weapon you know how to use, don't flash your money, and never — ever — ask anyone what they are shipping.
</TouristTip>

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={6} title="Prospect Point" category="settlement" image="/images/crusader/prospect-point.jpeg" imageAlt="The luxury villas of Prospect Point on Orison">

Prospect Point is Orison's most exclusive address — a collection of luxury villas and observation decks built on a platform that cost more than some small nations. The views are predictably spectacular, the kind of thing people pay fortunes for and then struggle to describe to anyone who hasn't been here. The architecture is what happens when you give a corporate architect an unlimited budget and a directive to "impress."

It is a good place to understand the two Crusaders that exist side by side: the one where people build cities in the sky, and the one where people profit from it. Both are impressive in their way. Neither is comfortable.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={7} title="Seraphim Station" category="practical" image="/images/crusader/seraphim-station.jpeg" imageAlt="The orbital Seraphim Station above Crusader">

Seraphim Station is the gateway to Crusader, and in its way, it is a destination in itself. The station manages the impossible task of handling traffic to and from a gas giant, and it does so with a kind of bureaucratic efficiency that borders on art. The medical facilities are excellent — word is, the doc here once treated a pilot who had tried to land on the "surface" of Crusader. She survived with a story and a permanent aversion to gas giants.

The real reason to stop here is the view. The station's observation deck looks down on Crusader's cloud tops, and if you time it right, you can watch a shipyard vessel being guided down through the clouds for its first test flight. It looks like a steel whale being born. The station staff will tell you they are used to it. They are lying.

</AttractionEntry>

## Eating and Drinking

Crusader has two very different dining cultures depending on which side of the atmosphere you are. Orison is a luxury experience — corporate wealth turned into cuisine. The orbital and surface stations are more utilitarian, serving the workers who keep the shipyards running and the traders passing through on their way to somewhere warmer.

### Orison — Cloudrest Retreat Dining

Cloudrest Retreat is the most exclusive residential platform on Orison, and the dining reflects the people who live there: expensive, understated, and almost impossible to walk past because the menus are designed to look like they weren't designed at all.

- **Cloudrest Dining Room** — The Retreat's own restaurant. It serves food that looks like it grew on a different planet. The menu changes with the rotation schedule of the shipyard workers below, as if the chef feels sorry for people who eat from freeze-dried packs all day. The wine list is the kind of thing people photograph and pretend not to. This is what eating on Crusader costs when the corporation that owns the air you breathe decides it owes you a meal.
- **The Empyrean Lounge** — More bar than restaurant, more restaurant than bar. Sits on the edge of Empyrean Park's main platform, which means the view does half the work. Cocktails are mixed with an attention to detail that Crusader Industries usually reserves for ship design. The quiet hours, roughly 11pm to 1am, are the best — the whole platform is just low music, the glow of Crusader through the windows, and the occasional conversation in a voice so low you have to lean in to hear it.

### Orison — Providence Platform

The industrial side of Orison doesn't do fancy. But the shipyard workers have been here long enough to know which cafés don't skimp on the coffee, and that knowledge has created a dining culture that is almost as complex as the gantries overhead.

- **Providence Cafeteria** — The shipyard cafeteria is the kind of place that could be worse. The food is warm, the portions are large, and the people who eat here are the most interesting in the city — engineers, welders, crane operators, and the occasional tourist who arrived without doing the research and has to pretend they were going here anyway. The coffee is good, the seating rotates fast, and the conversation is half technical, half complaint.
- **The Cloud Bar** — Named for its position on the outer edge of the Providence platform, it serves drinks to people who spend their days looking at ships they may never own. The beer is cold because the platform is cold, which means the heating is on even when the bar is empty. This is where contract discussions happen. Not the ones you sign — the ones you negotiate with yourself at 2am about whether you can afford the next part.

### Seraphim Station

The station is not a destination for dining. But it is the only place in the Stanton system where you can eat while watching a shipyard vessel being constructed, lifted through clouds, and lowered into atmospheric testing. The food court has a bar, a sandwich shop, and a hot food counter. It is adequate. It is adequate with a view.

- **Station Food Court** — Whatever shift rotation has produced. The seating faces the observation windows, which means the food is secondary and everyone knows it.
- **Seraphim Bar** — The station's only proper bar. It serves drinks to pilots who have just come up from the surface and people who are about to go down. Both groups have the same look: relief mixed with anticipation.

### Surface Outposts — Cellin, Daymar, Yela

The moons of Crusader are not tourist destinations in any conventional sense. But the occasional surface outpost has a bar or a cooking station that exists because the workers who built it decided they were worth something after a twelve-hour shift. These are not places you plan to visit. You find them by accident, usually at the end of a day when you are too cold to think about anything else and the only thing that matters is warm air and the knowledge that someone else is here.

- **GrimHEX Bar, Yela** — The Nine Tails pirate outpost has a bar that is, according to everyone who has visited, terrible. The stories are worse. But it is the only place in the Stanton system where you can drink with pirates while watching a gas giant rise over an asteroid field, and the only thing more honest than the whisky is the warning the bartender gives you about leaving. Bring a weapon you know how to use. Don't flash your money. Never ask anyone what they are shipping.

<TouristTip>
  Orison's dining is a study in contrasts: Cloudrest Retreat and Empyrean Park cater to the people who own the platforms, while the Providence Cafeteria and Cloud Bar serve the people who build and maintain them. If you want the full picture, visit both in the same day. The view from the industrial cafés — looking up at the luxury platforms — is almost as good as the view from the luxury platforms looking down.
</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

Crusader is a gas giant; you can't land on it in any conventional sense. All surface tourism happens on its moons — Orison, Yela, and Daymar — each with radically different environments. Your gear list depends entirely on which moon(s) you plan to visit, and since the most rewarding tours include more than one, you need gear that handles extremes: from breathable-but-windy luxury platforms to toxic, frigid wastelands.


**Universal gear (all Crusader moons):**

- **Multi-layer clothing system** — Each moon demands different layers. Orison is temperate; Yela is cold; Daymar is extreme. A modular system (base layer, insulating layer, outer shell) lets you reconfigure for each environment. Pack light but pack smart.
- **Sealed waterproof bag** — Rain on Orison is heavy; acid rain on Yela is corrosive; Daymar has abrasive dust that gets everywhere. Waterproof protection for electronics and documents is essential across all moons.
- **Personal first aid kit** — Medical facilities exist on Orison but are sparse on Yela and nearly nonexistent on Daymar. A comprehensive kit with bandages, antiseptic, painkillers, and prescription medications is a must.
- **Portable communications device with extended battery** — Long-range comms are essential on Yela and Daymar, where the distance from Orison means rescue is hours away, not minutes.
- **Sunscreen and UV protection** — Even on cloudy Orison, UV levels are high due to the gas giant's radiation. On Daymar, the reflected light from Crusader itself provides significant UV exposure.

**Orison-specific gear:**

- **Light rain jacket** — Orison's cloud cities are perpetually wet. A light, packable rain jacket keeps you dry without adding bulk. Umbrellas are impractical in the wind.
- **Comfortable walking shoes** — Cloudrest and Empyrean Park are walkable, but the platforms are long and the wind can make bare feet uncomfortable. Closed-toe shoes with grip are best.
- **Camera with lens cloth** — The constant moisture means your lens will fog and smudge. A microfiber cloth is as essential as any lens. The platforms are incredibly photogenic — make sure your camera survives the humidity.

**Yela-specific gear:**

- **Insulated jacket (rated to -20°C)** — Yela is cold, windy, and unforgiving. An insulated jacket with wind resistance is essential. The pirate bars have no heating, so you will feel every degree of drop when you leave the ship.
- **Head covering and gloves** — The wind on Yela is relentless. A balaclava or hood prevents heat loss and protects against the wind-chill that makes exposed skin painful. Gloves are mandatory for outdoor time.
- **Emergency beacon** — The distance from Orison means rescue is not fast. An emergency beacon with a range of at least 1,000 km is critical. Yela is rated 6/10 for danger, and the isolation is real.

**Daymar-specific gear:**

- **Full environmental suit** — Daymar's atmosphere is toxic and non-breathable (98.4% nitrogen). Standard suits are functional; rated environmental suits are the recommendation. The suit must handle both toxic air and abrasive regolith.
- **Thermal regulation pack** — Daymar swings from 78°C in direct sunlight to -40°C in shadow. Active thermal regulation is not optional — it is the difference between exploration and heat stroke (or hypothermia).
- **Emergency beacon and long-range comms** — Dust storms scramble short-range signals. Long-range comms (500+ km range) ensure you can call for help if the storm takes your QT lock.
- **Spare visor cleaner or microfiber cloth** — The regolith on Daymar adheres to everything. Visors fog, scratch, and cloud over. Clean your visor every 30–60 minutes or you will be navigating blind.

<TouristTip>
  Plan your moon stops carefully. Orison is accessible from any Stanton patch; Yela requires a dedicated flight (and the Nine Tails pirate presence means caution); Daymar is the most dangerous moon in the system and requires proper environmental suits. If you are doing all three moons in one trip, budget for a luggage transfer between Orison and the other moons — or pack for the harshest environment (Daymar) and compromise on comfort for Orison. The smartest approach: rent gear on Orison for Yela and Daymar from outfitters who deliver to all three moons.
</TouristTip>
