<AttractionEntry number={1} title="Lorville Workers District" category="settlement" image="/images/hurston/workers-district.jpeg" imageAlt="The gritty Workers District of Lorville, with the Central Tower looming in the background">

The heart of Lorville isn't the corporate headquarters — it's Leavsden Square, a cramped plaza in the Workers District where miners, haulers, and failed entrepreneurs trade stories and complaints. The buildings are cheap, pre-fabricated units stacked without care, and the whole place smells of recycled air and industrial waste. Yet this is where the city lives. M&V's Bar sits at the edge of the square, a dimly-lit dive where the beer is local and the music is worse. But the atmosphere is real. This is a place where conversations happen, where contracts are whispered over sticky tables, and where the bartender knows more about Hurston's underworld than the Hurston Security HQ.

The Central Tower looms over everything, a monument to corporate ego built on the backs of people who can't afford the view from the top. Walk from Leavsden to the strip mine boundary at night and listen to the machinery. It never stops. The sound becomes a kind of lullaby after a while — the soundtrack of a city that works itself to death.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="MacIntyre & Victor's Bar" category="hidden-find" image="/images/hurston/mv-bar.jpeg" imageAlt="Interior of the dimly-lit M&V's Bar">

Every city has a bar that should not be as good as it is. M&V's is Lorville's. It sits at the edge of the Workers District, tucked between a clothing outlet and a security checkpoint, an afterthought that became a destination. The decor is what happens when someone tries to recreate "industrial chic" on a budget that wouldn't cover the sound system. The drinks are local, which means cheap and strong, and the menu has never heard of a vegetable. But the people-watching is unmatched.

Come at shift change — roughly 6pm local — when miners fresh from the strip mines trade seats with haulers waiting for the next run to Everus Harbor. The conversations are loud, opinionated, and occasionally violent. The security guard at the door is ornamental. And the bartender? He has a memory for faces and a tolerance for stories that makes the place feel like a confessional. Just don't mention Hurston Dynamics unless you want to start an argument.

Anyone who claims to know the city will eventually mention Clovus Darneely. He operates out of the Reclamation & Disposal yard near the Workers District and has a line on contracts that Hurston Security wouldn't touch. Ask around. Someone will point you in the right direction, usually with a warning to watch your back.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={3} title="The Strip Mine Biomes" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/hurston/strip-mine.jpeg" imageAlt="Aerial view of the Hurston strip mines">

Hurston was a savannah once. Before Hurston Dynamics arrived, stripped it, and moved on to the next resource patch. What's left is a patchwork of devastation and strange, accidental beauty. The strip mine biomes are the most accessible — a landscape of exposed earth, industrial runoff, and machinery the size of buildings. At sunset, the sky goes the colour of rust and copper, and the pollution scatters the light into something that photographers call "the Hurston Glow." It is spectacular and deeply wrong.

The Strip Mine itself connects to the Workers District via the worker train line that runs past M&V's Bar. You can follow it on foot, though the security checkpoints are frequent and the guards are not known for their diplomacy. The mesa biomes are a different challenge — a maze of ship wreckage, industrial debris, and navigation hazards that make ground vehicle travel a test of patience. This is where the best salvage is, and where the most interesting photographs happen. Just don't expect to find soft ground.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={4} title="Maria Pure of Heart" category="practical" image="/images/hurston/maria-hospital.jpeg" imageAlt="The exterior of Maria Pure of Heart hospital in the Workers District">

Every city's hospital is a window into something. Maria Pure of Heart, in the Workers District, is a window into the cost of Hurston's economy. The waiting area is permanently full. The staff are overworked and underpaid. And the most common diagnosis is "respiratory distress from unregulated industrial exposure." Tourists don't usually end up here. But if you do, the experience is sobering in a way that Lorville's corporate propaganda never is.

It is named for someone who probably never existed, or existed in a way that made the branding possible. The irony is not lost on the nurses.

</AttractionEntry>

Lorville's most-stocked outfitter is a reminder that even in a company town, someone is making money on the side. ## Eating and Drinking

Hurston's dining scene can be summed up in a single word: contraband. On a planet where the air itself needs filtering, fresh food is the most honest currency you can hold. Everything served here is either imported — meaning expensive and sometimes sketchy — or grown in the kind of soil that would make a geologist weep. The result is a food culture that exists somewhere between desperation and dark comedy. The bars are where people come to forget they're eating in a corporate penitentiary, and the cafés are where people come to pretend they're on a different planet entirely.

### M&V's Bar — Workers District

Yes, it's already in the attractions. It belongs here too. M&V's serves whatever the local brewery has produced that week, which means the beer is cheap, strong, and occasionally dangerous — but the fried ration patties with hot sauce are genuinely good. The bartender has a recipe he will not share, but if you watch him closely at shift change, you'll notice he seasons with something that doesn't come in any official Hurston Dynamics inventory. The portions are generous, the tables are sticky, and the food is hot enough to make you sweat in a way that has nothing to do with the air quality. It is the closest thing Hurston has to a comfort meal.

### Teasa Spaceport Food Court

If you've just landed and your stomach is staging a revolt against whatever you ate on the trip in, the spaceport food court is the least-bad option available. It is exactly what you would expect: a cavernous hall of stainless steel and fluorescent lighting, with six vendors rotating their shifts and none of them proud of it. The noodle bar is marginally better than the meat stall, which is marginally better than the wrap counter, which is somewhere near the bottom but has the best seating. Don't eat anything that doesn't have a heat lamp above it. The coffee is terrible, but it is hot, and that is more than you can say for most of the beverage options.

### Leavsden Square Kiosks

The kiosks around Leavsden Square are where the workers of Lorville eat their lunch breaks — if they have the time. They are small, temporary, and operated by people who have figured out how to make something edible out of imported vegetables and whatever protein the supply chains can cough up. The best one is run by an older woman near the south end of the square who makes a stew that contains things the locals call "protein" and things nobody else calls anything at all. It costs almost nothing. It fills you up for the rest of the shift. Ask around. You'll find her by following the smell.

### The Strip Mine Canteen

A proper miner's canteen, located near the perimeter of the strip mine biomes. It exists because Hurston Dynamics knows that fed workers are more productive workers, and the food reflects this calculus: maximum caloric density, minimum flavor. The cafeteria is cavernous, industrial, and filled with the sound of heavy breathing and heavier machinery. The food is served in trays that cost more than you'd expect, which is a reminder that even the company cafeteria is priced like a hostage negotiation. The stew is hot, it is thick, and it will keep you working until your body breaks. This is not a place to come for a pleasant meal. It is a place to come when you are already at the strip mine and you realize you forgot to pack lunch.

### Tammany & Sons Cafeteria

A lesser-known feature of Lorville's most-stocked outfitter: a small cafeteria on the upper floor that sells sandwiches, soups, and the occasional hot meal to customers who have been browsing the filter masks and ship parts for long enough to get hungry. It is small, quietly run, and frequented by workers from nearby shops who have learned that Tammany's is one of the few places in Lorville that uses fresh bread. The owner, an off-duty Hurston Dynamics chef from Earth who ended up in Lorville for reasons nobody quite understands, makes a pastrami sandwich that is worth the climb up the stairs. It is also where you go if you want to eat somewhere quiet, away from the noise of the Workers District below.

### Shanty Town Cookouts

Beyond the perimeter gates, the shanty towns have a food culture born from necessity that is almost as vibrant as any official venue. The cooks are former miners, failed haulers, and people who got too good at improvising. They operate out of makeshift kitchens built from shipping containers, scavenged equipment, and whatever fuel they can scavenge. The food is unpredictable — sometimes brilliant, sometimes a hazard to your health — but it is always honest. You will eat alongside people whose stories you will not hear unless you sit with them long enough for the conversation to happen. Bring something to trade. Water is currency. Information is safer. The best cookouts happen at night, after the perimeter guards have settled into their shifts and the fires are visible through the haze.

### Aberdeen Penal Moon — Klescher Facility Dining Hall

If you are visiting Aberdeen on a tour, the Klescher facility will serve you food in its mess hall. It is not a dining experience. It is caloric maintenance delivered on a tray in a room at 230°C with a ceiling that looks like it might come down on you at any moment. The food is grey, the portions are adequate, and the taste is the kind of thing that exists only in the memories of people who have been here before and are trying to convince themselves it wasn't that bad. It is also the only place in the Stanton system where you can eat a meal at a temperature that would kill you in your suit. That, in itself, is a memorable experience.

<TouristTip>
  - **Air quality first**: Eat in filtered spaces whenever possible. The outdoor air in Lorville is a known respiratory hazard. Gyson Ready-Up softshell helmets from Tammany & Sons are available for visitors who need additional protection during mealtimes.
  - **Water matters**: Only drink bottled or treated water. The local supply, while technically potable, carries particulates that will not bother you today but will bother you next week. Bottled water is cheap here because Hurston has its own treatment plants.
  - **Budget for food**: Imported food is expensive on Hurston. A decent meal at a proper venue will set you back 50–150 UEC. Street food and kiosks run 10–30 UEC. Don't rely on the canteen food — it's functional, not enjoyable.
  - **Shift change at M&V's**: If you want the full experience, go at 6pm when the miners are fresh from the strip mines and the bar is at its most alive. The fried rations are worth the crowd.
</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

Hurston's atmosphere is technically breathable — which is the universe's way of saying "you won't die instantly, but you should still prepare." The air is heavily polluted with industrial particulates, and the surface is a mix of scorched savannah, industrial wasteland, and the occasional attempt at greenery that never quite makes it. The key challenge is respiratory health and heat management.


**Essential gear:**

- **Filter mask (minimum grade N95)** — Hurston's air quality is poor. The pollution from Hurston Dynamics' operations is visible, it tastes like metal, and it accumulates in your lungs over time. A filter mask from Tammany & Sons is the minimum; a Gyson Ready-Up softshell helmet is the investment that makes the difference between a visit you enjoy and a visit you regret.
- **Gyson Ready-Up softshell helmet** — Tammany & Sons' signature product and Hurston's unofficial uniform. The softshell helmet provides superior filtration and a degree of protection against the abrasive dust that gets everywhere. If you are staying on the surface for more than a day, this is worth every credit.
- **Moisture-wicking clothing** — The surface temperature on Hurston is not extreme, but the combination of industrial heat and humidity makes sweat management essential. Cotton is a mistake; moisture-wicking synthetics keep you comfortable and reduce the respiratory load of hot, humid air.
- **Sealed glasses or goggles** — The particulate count in Lorville and the Strip Mine biomes is high enough to irritate the eyes. Sealed glasses protect against both dust and the constant low-level chemical smell that permeates everything.
- **Portable water purifier** — While the local water supply is technically potable, the particulates it carries are a long-term health concern. A portable purifier lets you treat tap water or refill from any clean source.
- **Sunscreen and hat** — The Hurston sun is bright, and the polluted atmosphere does not provide meaningful UV filtration. In fact, the pollution can sometimes scatter UV in unexpected ways that increase exposure.

**Nice-to-have extras:**

- **Compact air purifier** — If you are staying in a hotel or rental, a small portable purifier makes a dramatic difference in indoor air quality. The hotel rooms in Lorville are not poorly maintained, but they were not designed for respiratory health.
- **Breath analyzer app** — Some pilots use their ship's sensor suite or a portable breath analyzer to monitor their exposure. It is not essential but provides useful data if you are staying on the surface for an extended period.
- **Portable charger** — The Gyson helmet and any powered filtration devices draw significant power. A portable charger ensures your equipment does not die mid-day.
- **Compass and map** — The Strip Mine biomes and the Shanty Towns are not well-mapped. A physical compass and a printed map are reliable backups to digital navigation, which can be unreliable in industrial zones with heavy EMF interference.
- **Small first aid kit with inhaler** — Respiratory issues are the most common medical problem on Hurston. An inhaler (even for non-asthmatics, as a precaution) is wise. A basic first aid kit covers minor cuts and scrapes from the industrial terrain.

<TouristTip>
  The air quality on Hurston is not something you can "get used to." It gets worse the longer you stay. If you have any pre-existing respiratory conditions, consult your doctor before visiting and bring prescribed medication. The Gyson helmet is not a fashion choice — it is the single most important piece of gear you can bring to this planet. Budget 150–300 UEC for a quality mask or helmet from Tammany & Sons if you forgot to bring one.
</TouristTip>

Beyond the six perimeter gates, where the no-fly-zone ends and the desert begins, the shanty towns are a reminder that Hurston's economy has losers as well as winners. Makeshift structures, built from whatever could be salvaged from the strip mines, house workers whose contracts expired, whose health failed, or who simply could not meet the productivity quotas that Hurston Dynamics demands.

The security presence is lighter here. So is the enforcement of Hurston's laws. The people who live in the shanty towns have a particular hospitality for outsiders, born from a shared experience of being on the wrong side of a line someone else drew. If you go, bring something to trade. Water is currency. Information is safer.
