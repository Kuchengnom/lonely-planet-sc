<AttractionEntry number={1} title="The First River" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/microtech/ships-on-lake.jpeg" imageAlt="Ships parked at the edge of the first river lake basin">

On a planet where exposed skin freezes in minutes, finding open water feels like a trick. The river is no trick. It cuts through a thawed valley in the northern hemisphere with an absolute indifference to the permafrost surrounding it on every side, and geologists in the game's lore have never produced a satisfying explanation for why it exists.

From **New Babbage**, point your ship to heading **205** and quantum toward **MT DataCenter E2Q-NSG-Y**. Cancel the jump when the remaining distance to the data centre reads about **900 km**. Below you, look for a dark strip where the white breaks — a thawed valley of wet rock, scrub, and open water. Descend slowly. The river is easy to miss at speed and impossible to find once you've passed it.

The lake basin at the river's widest point is the most photographed spot. Land on the bank and walk to the water's edge. It is, genuinely, one of the stranger moments Stanton has to offer.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={2} title="The Canyon River" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/microtech/canyon-river-aerial.jpeg" imageAlt="Aerial view of the canyon river winding through autumn forest" href="/lonely-planet-sc/stanton/microtech/northern-wastes/canyon-river">

The newer discovery is harder to reach than the first river, but it rewards the detour with canyon walls, a deeper colour palette, and an approach that feels like genuine exploration rather than a nav-point run.

Quantum to **MT DataCenter KH3-AAE-L**, then hold heading **027** and fly roughly **440 km** while maintaining altitude. Watch the terrain shift from uniform snowfield to something with structure — ridgelines, cuts, deeper shadow. When the canyon shape becomes clear from above, slow down and descend along its length. Two alternate fixes if you lose it: search the planet-facing midpoint between **OM-6** and **OM-3**, or start from **Dunboro** settlement and fly heading **265** for about **60 km**.

The canyon itself is wide enough to fly a small ship through at low speed. Do that before you land.

</AttractionEntry>

<ExplorerView slot="explorer" author="Survey Pilot, New Babbage">
The trick on microTech is patience. You're going to fly right over what you're looking for at least once — the rivers are invisible from height and the storms don't respect your schedule. The moment you decide to turn back is usually the moment the weather clears. Stay low on the return leg and watch the terrain change beneath you. That is when you find the things that aren't on any guide.
</ExplorerView>

<AttractionEntry number={3} title="The Frozen Lakes" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/microtech/ice-arch.jpeg" imageAlt="Ice arch formation rising from a frozen lake on MicroTech">

Scattered across the sub-polar latitudes, microTech's frozen lakes are a different kind of stillness from the rivers — no anomaly, no mystery, just flat white expanses carved by a planet doing exactly what physics requires of it. What makes them worth landing for are the formations the ice builds on top of itself over time: arches, columns, and abstract blocks of compressed blue-white that look like sculpture and have no practical explanation beyond weather.

The ice-arch formations are the most photographed features on the surface after the rivers. They appear most reliably in the shallower lake regions where wind sculpts the ice before it's buried by snow. You can walk them in an appropriate suit — the footing is uneven and the ice closer to the water's edge is unreliable, but the views are worth the caution.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={4} title="The Bioluminescent Caves" category="hidden-find" image="/images/microtech/bioluminescent-cave.jpeg" imageAlt="Bioluminescent cave ceiling covered in blue-cyan glowing organisms">

Ask any explorer who has stumbled into one of these and they will use the same word: otherworldly. Shallow cave systems across the surface host ceilings and walls dotted with blue-cyan bioluminescent growth — thousands of pinpoint lights in complete darkness that make the rock above you look like inverted starfield.

They are not on any official map, which is half the appeal. Finding one requires low-altitude flying across the tundra biome and watching the terrain for dark openings in cliff faces and hillsides. Enter slowly — the caves are not deep but the floor is uneven and the dark is absolute beyond your suit lights. This is one for pilots who fly without a particular destination in mind and end up somewhere they didn't expect.

<TouristTip>
  Approach any cave opening from the side before committing your ship. The entrance height can be deceptive from above, and some openings large enough to enter on foot are too narrow for a Pisces. Park outside and walk in.
</TouristTip>

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={5} title="New Babbage" category="settlement" image="/images/microtech/flying-over-mountains.jpeg" imageAlt="Small exploration ship flying over MicroTech's snowy mountain ranges toward New Babbage">

The only city on microTech is a marvel of corporate ambition and a small mercy after wilderness flying. The towers are too tall for a planet this cold, the plazas too clean to feel lived-in, and the whole place has the quality of a concept render that was built without anyone checking whether people would actually enjoy it. And yet, after a long survey flight with deteriorating weather and a near-empty fuel tank, the lights of **New Babbage** on the horizon are the most welcome sight on the planet.

The **Commons** shopping area at ground level is worth a slow walk — coffee, gear, and an architectural grandeur that borders on absurdist. If you visit between December and January, the enormous **Christmas Tree** that appears in the main plaza is one of the game's most earnest and unexpectedly human moments: a massive festive decoration on a corporate ice planet, erected for no apparent reason beyond the fact that someone at microTech thought it was a good idea.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={6} title="Surface Outposts" category="settlement" image="/images/microtech/surface-outpost.jpeg" imageAlt="Ruined surface outpost buried in snow among pine trees">

The ruined outposts scattered through the snow-pine forests are part crash site, part mystery, and entirely worth a detour if you are already flying the wilderness. The antenna arrays, structural frames, and scattered equipment suggest long-term habitation; most of them look like they were abandoned in a hurry and never returned to.

**Dunboro** is the most accessible starting point — reachable from **MT DataCenter KH3-AAE-L** on heading **265** for roughly **60 km** — and functions as a working outpost rather than a ruin, making it a useful waypoint between the canyon river and the return leg to New Babbage. Smaller unnamed crash sites appear throughout the tundra with no nav markers. Land next to the antenna towers and have a walk around.

</AttractionEntry>

<AttractionEntry number={7} title="The Forest Biomes" category="natural-wonder" image="/images/microtech/canyon-forest.jpeg" imageAlt="Autumn forest biome on MicroTech with orange foliage and rocky outcrops">

At the latitudes where snow gives way to tundra, the landscape changes in a way that photographs struggle to convey. The trees are frost-edged pine, dense enough to crowd the sky, and the ground between them is covered in orange-red autumn undergrowth that makes the whole scene look as though it's caught mid-season — which, on a planet with microTech's orbital mechanics, it effectively always is.

The canyon river cuts through the centre of the richest forest biome on the planet, which is the secondary reason to fly that route slowly. This is where microTech stops looking like a hostile environment and starts looking like somewhere a person might actually want to be. If you have a small ship with good atmospheric handling, drop below the treeline and follow the forest contours south. The transition back to snowfield is abrupt enough that the exact line feels like a boundary someone drew.

</AttractionEntry>

## Eating and Drinking

microTech does not do wilderness dining. The cold simply does not permit it — you eat where the heating works and the food hasn't spent the last six hours frozen solid. That means the dining scene is concentrated entirely inside New Babbage's domes and at Port Tressler in orbit. Both are corporate affairs: clean, efficient, and forgettable on purpose. But there are a few places worth knowing about.

### New Babbage — The Commons

The Commons (nicknamed the "double bubble") is the main commercial and social hub of New Babbage, located in the smaller of the two merged domes. It's modelled after a douchey campus with recreational space, and the eateries ring a large central yoga field.

- **Twyn's Sandwiches** — The most reliable quick bite on the planet. Sandwiches and energy drinks, nothing more, nothing less. It's fast, it's warm, and it won't make you regret your life choices at 2 AM. The bread is surprisingly good for a place that prides itself on efficiency over craft.
- **Ellroy's** — Ice cream, fresh fruit, and water. On a frozen planet, Ellroy's is a bizarrely wonderful contradiction: a shop selling things that are both cold and exotic. The ice cream is a tourist favourite — part novelty, part necessity. Nobody questions the appeal of eating ice cream on a world where exposed skin freezes in minutes.
- **Garcia's Greens** — Smoothies and fresh fruit. The freshest produce in a hemisphere that grows very little, and you can taste the difference the moment you walk in. It's expensive because shipping fruit to the coldest world in Stanton costs more than shipping it to the rest of the system combined.

### New Babbage — The Plaza

Adjacent to the Commons on the larger part of the double bubble, the Plaza is the corporate business park. Here you'll find microTech's Factory Line outlet and several cafés aimed at commuters who need caffeine and don't have time to think about it.

- **microTech Factory Line** — Corporate merch and convenience items. The attached café is the kind of place that exists because its employees need somewhere to go between meetings, but the coffee is decent and the seating is warm.

### Port Tressler (Orbital)

The orbital station above New Babbage has a full food court, a bar, and a hot dog shop. Port Tressler is where you come for the views of the planet below and the knowledge that nobody is going to try to sell you a weapon.

- **Food Court** — The full station food court with whatever the shift rotation has turned up that day. It's not a destination, but it's reliable, and the portions are generous.
- **Bar** — A proper bar with seating that doesn't double as a workstation. This is where pilots come when they've spent too many hours in the atmosphere and need something that tastes like it was made by people who aren't trying to optimise your caloric intake.
- **Hot Dog Shop** — Yes, really. A hot dog shop on the coldest planet in Stanton. The hot dogs are heated with the same engineering obsession that built the planet's infrastructure, and somehow, that means they're actually quite good.

<TouristTip>
  Bring a jacket to Port Tressler's food court — the station's heating is excellent, but the walk from the docking ring is long and the corridor temperatures are "corporate neutral," not "I want to linger." New Babbage's domes are heated to a comfortable 18–20 °C; the Commons and Plaza are always warm, always lit, and always full of people who don't want to be outside.
</TouristTip>

## What to Bring

microTech does not forgive poor preparation. The cold is not a suggestion — it is a constant, patient threat. Exposed skin freezes in under a minute at surface temperatures that regularly drop below -100°C. If your gear list isn't thorough, your visit will be brief and uncomfortable.

<ExplorerView slot="explorer" author="MicroTech Surface Surveyor, New Babbage">
The first rule of microTech: bring twice the thermal protection you think you need, because the cold does not care what you think you need. I have seen seasoned pilots come back with gear that was rated "adequate" and discover — too late — that "adequate" means "you will survive but you will remember this forever." Bring more than enough. The difference in price between a suit rated to -80°C and one rated to -150°C is nothing compared to the cost of a rescue call you didn't want to make.
</ExplorerView>

**Essential cold-weather gear:**

- **Thermal survival suit (rated to -150°C minimum)** — Standard EVA suits are technically functional but will struggle during prolonged surface operations. The difference between "functional" and "comfortable" on microTech is the difference between exploring for four hours and two. The best survival suits on the market are rated to well below what microTech actually reaches, which is exactly the point: bring a margin.
- **Heated suit liner (recommended)** — A battery-powered thermal layer that clips inside your suit. On microTech, this is not a luxury; it is a life support device. Battery life varies by brand but should be rated for at least 8 hours of continuous use at maximum output.
- **Thermal goggles with anti-fog coating** — Breath condensation will freeze on your visor faster than you can wipe it. Anti-fog coatings are essential. The blinding effect of sunlight reflecting off ice and snow means polarized lenses are also strongly recommended — the glare alone can cause visual fatigue that ruins an entire day of exploration.
- **Emergency beacon with extended-range antenna** — microTech's ice storms regularly cause pilots to lose visual and quantum lock with their ships. Beacon range should exceed 500 km. The cold does not just make finding your way home difficult; it makes the *act of getting help* difficult because your ship may have also been immobilized by ice accumulation.
- **Thermal-insulated water flask** — Standard water bladders freeze solid within minutes of surface exposure. A vacuum-insulated flask keeps water drinkable for hours and can double as a heat source in an emergency. Fill it before you land.
- **Insulated survival rations** — Food that can be eaten without heating, or rations that can be warmed in a suit-integrated heater. Chocolate and energy bars that don't melt (or freeze into projectiles) are ideal. The caloric demand on a microTech surface tour is significantly higher than on a temperate world because your body is fighting cold the entire time.

**Nice-to-have extras:**

- **Drone with thermal camera** — Ice caves, thermal vents, and some ruined outposts are easier to spot from the air with thermal imaging. A drone lets you scout a large area before committing your ship to a slow, dangerous descent through poor visibility.
- **Shotgun** — Not for the cold, but for the occasional hostile wildlife that has adapted to microTech's surface life. The local fauna includes hardy arthropods and the occasional escaped livestock. The shotgun handles both and serves as a starting signal if your beacon fails.
- **Polarizing filter for camera** — Ice and snow create extreme glare. A polarizer cuts reflections and reveals detail that is otherwise invisible, turning a white-out photograph into a rich, textured image. The canyon river and bioluminescent caves are dramatically enhanced by polarized shots.
- **Traction boots with micro-spikes** — New Babbage's sidewalks are treated, but anything outside the city is uncontrolled ice. A single misstep on a glacier or ice arch is not a minor inconvenience.
- **Compact snow shovel** — If your ship lands in fresh snow and gets bogged, a shovel clears a landing zone faster than patience. The outposts in the tundra sometimes have shallow snow that hides obstacles — clear a path before you walk.

<TouristTip>
  The single most common microTech rescue reason: pilots who underestimate the cold with inadequate thermal suits. The planet's temperature regularly drops below -100°C, and most standard EVA suits start degrading in performance well above that. If you are doing a full-day surface tour of the rivers and caves, budget for a proper thermal suit rental from New Babbage outfitters. It is cheaper than a rescue and significantly more comfortable.
</TouristTip>
